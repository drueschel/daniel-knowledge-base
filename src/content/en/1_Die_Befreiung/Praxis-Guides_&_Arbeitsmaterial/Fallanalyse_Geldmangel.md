# Case analysis: solving a lack of money
*How chronic lack of money acts as an unconscious survival strategy and how to resolve it using the Gopal method.*

No matter how often we approach the topic of money on a logical level (saving, working more, investing) - if there is unresolved Attachment Trauma behind it, the nervous system will always ensure that the lack remains. In the Gopal method, lack of money is **never the actual problem**, but always a reflection of the relationship and bonding level.

---

## 1. From the symptom to the level of attachment
In our society, money is a symbol of energy, security and the right to exist. If the nervous system has learned in childhood that one's existence is threatened or there is a chronic lack of love, the mind later shifts this problem to money.

Why? Because the pain on the attachment level would be too threatening. A problem with money appears to the mind as "solvable" (you just apparently have to work more). This keeps us busy all the time and we don't have to feel the actual pain.

Depending on the type of bond, lack of money is used completely differently:

---

## 2. Lack of money in merger type
The Merging Type learned in childhood: *"There is not enough love/connection, I have to make an extreme effort to get some of it."*

*   **The mechanism:** The chronic lack of emotional connection is transferred 1:1 to the bank account. No matter how much money comes in, the feeling of "it's not enough" remains.
*   **The self-sabotage:** He unconsciously sabotages his financial success (e.g. through sudden unexpected expenses or bad contracts) in order to maintain the usual inner state of "lack" and "I have to fight". This is, paradoxically, the “safe” and familiar state for his nervous system.
*   **The Projection:** "Life (or the boss/customers) isn't giving me enough. I'm being treated unfairly."

**The solution (reality check):**
The merger must bring the deficiency back into the relationship. He needs to stop fighting for money and instead communicate honestly in direct contact:
> *"My head is thinking that life isn't giving me enough."*
> *"I feel pressure in my body."*
> *"I feel extremely needy, helpless and sad."*

---

## 3. Lack of money in the Autonomy Type
The Autonomy Type learned in childhood: *"There is no one there. My very existence is threatened."*

*   **The mechanism:** For the Autonomy Type, lack of money is not just "lack", but an **existential death threat**. The lack of money reflects the deep childish feeling that one's own existence is not secure.
*   **The self-sabotage:** He unconsciously creates the lack of money himself in order to be able to continuously work on this abstract problem. As long as he fights against the lack of money, he seems to be fighting for his survival. This perfectly distracts him from the fact that he actually lives in complete isolation.
*   **The projection:** "If I just finally have enough money, I will be safe and allowed to exist (without needing people)."

**The solution (reality check):**
The Autonomy Type must recognize that the feeling of existential threat is a childish illusion. Healing happens when he lets go of the fight for money and dares to come into contact with his fear of death (e.g. with a therapist):
> *"I'm having existential panic."*
> *"Do you exist? Can I be here even if I don't provide any money/service?"*

---

## 4. Conclusion & Getting out of the loop
As soon as you notice that you are worrying about money again or slipping into existential fears, be aware: **Your nervous system is currently acting up.**

1. **Stop trying to solve the external money problem logically** (at this moment).
2. **Recognize the shift:** "Ah, my system is mirroring my Attachment Trauma right now."
3. **Turn to Contact:** Go to your partner, a friend, or your therapist and use *honest sharing*. Allow the pain of lack of connection or existential dread as you feel that there is someone there to hear you today.

When the nervous system experiences that the bond is secure today, the abstract construct "lack of money" immediately loses its necessity as a survival strategy.
