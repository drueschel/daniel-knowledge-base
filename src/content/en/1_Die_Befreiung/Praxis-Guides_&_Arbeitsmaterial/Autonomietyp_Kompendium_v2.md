# Autonomy Type Compendium - Guide for Therapists (Gopal's Method)

This document provides an extremely structured, simple and clear guide for therapists to work effectively with the **Autonomy Type** in a session. It brings together the latest findings on the origin, the mental mechanism and the practical solution.

---

## 1. Summary of symptoms

The Autonomy Type shows specific behavior patterns and symptoms in everyday life and in relationships that indicate a deep attachment disorder:

*   **Withdrawal and isolation:** The first impulse upon contact is “let’s go”. The client avoids closeness and flees into isolation.
*   **Self-extinction:** During contact, the client acts as if he or she does not exist. Your own needs and feelings are completely suppressed or not felt.
*   **Escape into substitute counterpart:** The energy is directed inwards. Escape into abstract worlds of thought (science), your own body (sport, illness) or into non-personal spiritual spaces.
*   **Symptom lack of money:** Experienced as an existential threat. The client torpedoes his own success in order to be constantly preoccupied with the problem and avoid the real conflict (contact).
*   **Symptom Stress & Burnout:** An abstract "working yourself to death" to justify your own existence ("I work myself away in the hope of being allowed to exist").
*   **Split core feeling:** Deep loneliness and infinite sadness, which are often only felt as a short impulse in the moment of withdrawal (moving away).
*   **Extreme fears:** In severe cases, contact leads to real panic or fear of death, as closeness is linked to an existential loss of control.

---

## 2. Mechanism: From the symptom to the attachment level

**Origin in childhood:**
The child experienced an environment in which no real emotional contact was possible - either through complete neglect (no one was there) or massive emotional abuse (the child's needs were completely ignored). The child concluded: *"There is no counterpart. I have to survive completely alone."*

**Today's creation through thoughts (re-enactment):**
Today there is no real threat anymore, but the Autonomy Type recreates the Attachment Trauma every day through their own state of mind. The mechanism works as follows:

1.  **Distancing as Protection:** The client continually creates distance to protect himself from the painful (perceived) realization that no one is there.
2.  **The toxic basic assumptions (illusions):**
    *   *Illusion 1:* There is no one there. Contact is basically impossible.
    *   *Illusion 2:* My very existence is the problem. When I show my needs (loneliness, sadness), the other person disappears immediately.
    *   *Illusion 3:* Other people are overpowering and independent, but in reality they are completely frozen and dissociated inside.
3.  **The paradox:** In order to be allowed to exist and (unconsciously) enable contact, the Autonomy Type cancels itself out in contact. He shifts his attention completely into abstract spaces, which means that no real contact can actually occur.

---

## 3. The solution & questioning technique (The Flow)

The transformation does not happen by analyzing the symptom (e.g. money or burnout), but by confronting the mind with today's real attachment situation.

**Establish physical security (if you have severe fears):**
If the client feels panic during the contact:

1.  **Sensory check:** Ask the client to scan the room with their eyes: *"Are your life in acute danger at this moment? Are you being physically threatened?"*
2.  **Movement & Focus:** Keep the body in slight movement. Focus the mind on *future plans* to interrupt the pull of trauma (freezing/dissociation).

**Use the entrance door:**
Bring the client's attention to the moment when he or she decides to retreat (the "away movement").

*   *Instructions:* "When you take yourself out, feel carefully. What is there?"
*   *Goal:* Making you aware of the deep, underlying **sadness**. Give space to this grief.

**The reality check (the questioning technique):**
The client must compare his/her toxic basic assumptions directly with the other person (the therapist or life partner). The client should ask these questions **exactly** and force themselves to actually hear the answer.

*   **Question 1:** *"Are you there? Are you there?"*
    *(Aims at Illusion 1: Nobody is there)*

*   **Question 2:** *"Is it true that you would have a problem if I told you that I feel lonely and sad and that I exist? Would you have a problem with that?"*
    *(Aims at Illusion 2: My Needs Drive You Away)*

*   **Question 3:** *"Is it true that you feel overwhelmingly powerful and completely independent of me and in reality you are frozen and dissociated inside? Is that true?"*
    *(Aims at Illusion 3: The False Image of the Other)*

**Action & Integration:**
*   The client must force himself into contact (despite inner resistance that incorrectly signals withdrawal as the “right thing”).
*   Every movement towards it, no matter how the conversation ends, is already success and transformation.
