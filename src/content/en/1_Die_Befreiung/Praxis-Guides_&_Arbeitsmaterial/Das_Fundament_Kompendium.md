# Basics & Practice: The Foundation

The foundation of Gopal's work is based on a fundamental shift in perspective and understanding how our nervous system responded to early relationship experiences. This practical guide combines the essential core principles from the module “The Foundation” with the specific therapy guide. It serves as a structured guide to uncover unconscious attachment patterns (the "scene pushing") and to transform them through direct reality checks.

## 1. The basic principle: Everything is fine with you
The most important basic assumption for therapeutic work is: **There is nothing about you that needs to be repaired or optimized.**

The current suffering (be it physical symptoms, relationship problems or circumstances such as lack of money) arises from a **distortion of perception**.

- **The Metaphor of the Prism:** A healthy nervous system is like a pure prism through which light (reality) is naturally refracted. Childhood traumas act like impacts into this prism. The incoming light is distorted, constructing bizarre and painful realities outside.
- **Symptoms as signposts:** The external problems are not the actual problem, but merely the compass that points to the unresolved attachment conflict.

## 2. The universal healing flow
Today we no longer suffer our attachment traumas from childhood through external threats, but rather we actively create them ourselves through our own thoughts and projections. The healing path always follows this pattern:

1. **Recognize the symptom:** What external problem is showing up? (Lack of money, burnout, relationship problems).
2. **Translate to attachment:** The symptom is always a mirror for the relationship level (lack of external connection = perceived lack of emotional connection).
3. **Personal contribution & protection:** How does the client create this distance himself today? What does he want to protect himself from?
4. **Uncover the illusion/projection:** What unconscious, false image does the client have of the other person?
5. **The reality check (the core questions):** The child's deepest beliefs must be checked in reality by asking the other person (partner/therapist) direct questions. Listening to the answer updates the nervous system.

## 3. Recognizing your own type: The first impulse
When a child grows up in an unsuitable environment, he or she essentially has only two survival strategies (fusion or autonomy). In order to find out in which direction you are leaning, it is important to observe the **first, unconscious impulse** when you come into contact with people (e.g. on the street):

- Is your first impulse to move over and make contact? -> **Merger**
- Is your first impulse to escape and distance (“Let’s get away”)? -> **Autonomy**

**Beware of the countermovement:**
Often the mind develops a countermovement to our actual life that can lead us astray:

- A Merging Type who is constantly in contact and running after people is often exhausted by this and dreams of traveling around the world alone or isolating themselves. But that doesn't make him an autonomous type, since he doesn't live this withdrawal in reality.
- An isolated, autonomous type who lives alone in his apartment unconsciously longs for contact and may dream of complete unity (or seek it through drugs). That doesn't make him a Merging Type.

What matters is not what you dream about or how you internally compensate for the situation, but rather what your life actually looks like now.

## 4. The practice: honest communication & regulation
When one's own type and unconscious projections have been recognized through the "First Impulse" (see above), the nervous system needs a new, safe experience in the present. The central tool for this is **honest sharing**.

The goal is to get out of the unconscious "scene pushing" (constantly talking about external problems) and instead communicate the inner state directly and without judgment. This happens through the strict separation of three levels:

1. **Thoughts:** "My head is thinking that..." (This defuses projections).
2. **Feelings:** "I'm feeling right now..." (anger, sadness, joy, fear, disgust).
3. **Body sensations:** "In my body I feel..." (pressure, tingling, width, tightness).

This type of communication immediately creates presence and security in the nervous system of both conversation partners.

### Dealing with resistance in practice
If you take in this information and feel inner resistance, that is a **very good sign**. It means that the information has reached your depths and something is starting to move. Don't try to fight the resistance, take it with you.

**Important note for therapeutic work:**
The transformation does not lie in intellectual understanding. The client must express their specific core questions (the reality check) and **really listen**. The nervous system often rejects the response. Honest communication ("I've just noticed that my head doesn't believe you") is the key to getting back into contact.
