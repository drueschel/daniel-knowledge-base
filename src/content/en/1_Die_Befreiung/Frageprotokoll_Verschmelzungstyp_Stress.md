# Question protocol: Dissolve stress & burnout (Merging Type)
*A template for the reality check using the Gopal method*

This protocol is designed specifically for you to go through step by step with a safe counterpart (partner, friend, therapist). The goal is to expose your current “stress” (working away, constantly worrying) as an unconscious strategy that you actually use to buy closeness because deep down you are afraid of being abandoned.

---

## Preparation (The rules of the game for both of you)
Before you start, briefly clarify the general conditions:

*   **For your counterpart:** The only task is to listen to you and answer your questions (in phase 3) **honestly, briefly and without long explanations**. No discussion, no advice, no comforting.
*   **For you:** Your job is to **really let the answers sink in** (listen!). Your mind will try to fight off the answers (“They just say that”). Try to notice this and let yourself fall into your body.

---

## Phase 1: Transfer the stress to the relationship
*Stress in the Merging Type is almost always "I care too much about others because I'm subconsciously afraid that the bond will otherwise break."*

**Read the following sentence to the person you are talking to (or yourself) and fill in the blank:**
> "The area in which I am currently straining, stressing and overtaxing myself the most is..." *(e.g. at work, organizing the household, with friends...)*

**Now recognize the real reason (the bond level):**
> "I feel that I'm actually trying so hard because deep down I'm afraid that..." *(e.g. ...you'll reject me, I'm not good enough, you'll leave me).*

---

## Phase 2: Uncover the illusion about the other person
*You expend an incredible amount of energy (stress) because you have a very specific - but wrong - image of the other person in your head.*

**Be honest about what your nervous system subconsciously believes:**
> "If I'm being completely honest, I often feel deep down that you can't cope without my help or without my care. Part of me sees you as small, weak, or dependent on me."

*(Let that sink in for a moment. Saying it is often the first big release).*

---

## Phase 3: The Reality Check (The 3 Core Questions)
*Now you confront your projections with reality. Ask these exact questions and listen to the answers.*

**Question 1 (Testing the fear of loss):**
> *"Are you planning to leave me?"*
*(Hear the answer. Let it sink in).*

**Question 2 (Testing suppressed autonomy):**
> *"Do you have a problem with me telling you that I'm just exhausted or angry right now and doing my own thing (not caring about you)?"*
*(Hear the answer. If the answer is "No", it means: Your autonomy is safe).*

**Question 3 (Shattering the Projection):**
> *"Is it true that you feel small, helpless and dependent on me?"*
*(Listen carefully. This is the moment that matters).*

---

## Phase 4: The Projection Break (The Key Moment)
If the other person answers question 3 (ideally something like: *"No! I don't need this constant care from you. I don't need anything from you"*), then pay attention to what is happening inside you.

Shattering this illusion often creates a small internal shock. The “scene pushing” collapses. Suddenly it becomes noticeable: you didn't have to create all the stress.

**What do you feel now?** (Most of the time the true, underlying feeling arises now: e.g. deep sadness, emptiness, fear or anger).

---

## Phase 5: The honest sharing (The Real Connection)
*Now that the backdrop is gone, you can have real contact. Simply share what is going on with you at this moment.*

**Use the sentences of honest communication (without accusation, just about yourself):**

*   *"I feel (in my body)..."* (e.g. pressure on my chest, my heart beating)
*   *"I'm feeling..."* (e.g. angry, sad, helpless, extremely lonely)
*   *"My head is thinking that..."* (e.g. you're not serious anyway / that I don't know who I am without the stress).

**Result:** The stress/burnout urge should dissipate in this moment because the nervous system has now created the longed-for closeness through truth (instead of through strenuous worrying).
