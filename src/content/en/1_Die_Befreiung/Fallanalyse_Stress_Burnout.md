# Case analysis: Dissolve stress & burnout
*How chronic stress and exhaustion act as an unconscious survival strategy and how to resolve them through the Gopal Method.*

No matter how often we approach the issue of stress on a logical level (time management, vacations, fewer tasks) - if there is unresolved Attachment Trauma behind it, the nervous system will always ensure that the overload persists. In the Gopal method, stress and burnout are **never the actual problem**, but always a reflection of the relationship and bonding level.

---

## 1. From the symptom to the level of attachment
In our society, stress is an almost recognized symbol of importance and performance. However, if the nervous system has learned in childhood that one's existence or connection to one's parents is threatened, the mind later postpones this problem to work or everyday life.

Why? Because the pain on the attachment level would be too threatening. A problem with stress appears to the mind as “solvable” or “normal”. This keeps us busy all the time and we don't have to feel the actual pain - the loneliness or the fear of loss.

Depending on the type of attachment, stress is used completely differently:

---

## 2. Stress & burnout in the Merging Type
The Merging Type learned in childhood: *"I will only be loved and not abandoned if I conform, take care of myself, and suppress my own needs (such as anger or autonomy)."*

*   **The Mechanism:** The constant stress comes from the endless attempt to maintain control over the relationship. The merger self-sacrificingly takes care of his partner, family or colleagues (helper syndrome) in order to make himself indispensable.
*   **Self-sabotage:** He keeps creating new tasks for himself and burdening himself with other people's problems. The burnout is the result of him constantly occupying the other person's space in order to force closeness, thereby completely burning his own life energy.
*   **The projection:** "The other person is weak, small and helpless. Without me, everything here collapses."

**The solution (reality check):**
The merger must bring the stress back into the relationship. He needs to stop caring about others and instead communicate honestly in direct contact:
> *"My head is thinking that you can't cope without me."*
> *"I feel total exhaustion in my body."*
> *"I feel extremely anxious and angry."*

---

## 3. Stress & burnout in the autonomous type
The Autonomy Type learned in childhood: *"There is no one there. My mere existence is the problem. If I show myself, the other person turns away."*

*   **The mechanism:** For the autonomous type, stress is a way to gain a reason to exist. He doesn't care about others, but works himself to death in abstract systems (career, science, technology).
*   **Self-sabotage:** He unconsciously creates the absolute overload himself. As long as he achieves extreme things on the objective level, he unconsciously hopes that he can still exist. Overworking is the perfect shield to avoid real emotional contact.
*   **The projection:** "I am only allowed to be here if I function continuously and perform extremely well."

**The solution (reality check):**
The Autonomy Type must realize that this type of performance is a childish illusion in order to avoid loneliness. The healing happens when he stops performing and ventures into contact with his true isolation:
> *"I'm having existential panic."*
> *"Do you exist? Can I be here even if I'm not doing anything?"*

---

## 4. Conclusion & Getting out of the loop
As soon as you notice that you are massively stressed again or that you are heading towards burnout, be aware: **Your nervous system is currently acting up.**

1. **Stop trying to solve the external stress problem logically** (at this moment).
2. **Recognize the shift:** "Ah, my system is currently reflecting my Attachment Trauma (fear of loss or existential fear)."
3. **Turn to Contact:** Go to your partner, a friend, or your therapist and use *honest sharing*. Allow the pain of fear of abandonment or complete isolation as you feel that there is someone there to hear you today.

When the nervous system experiences that the attachment is secure today, the abstract construct "stress" immediately loses its necessity as a survival strategy.
