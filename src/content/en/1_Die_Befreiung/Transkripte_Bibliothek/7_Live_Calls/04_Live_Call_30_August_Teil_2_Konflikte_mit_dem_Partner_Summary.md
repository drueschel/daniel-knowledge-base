# Live Call: August 30 – Part 2 (Conflicts with Partner)

> 📜 **Full Verbatim Transcript (49:20 Min.):** [Read the full transcript of this call](./Volltranskripte/04_Live_Call_30_August_Teil_2_Konflikte_mit_dem_Partner_Volltranskript)

## 1. Initial Situation & Symptom
The client has been married for roughly nine years in a relationship characterized by recurring, exhausting arguments, conflicts, and an ongoing state of inner warfare. Whenever differences arise, she experiences intense internal dread and launches preemptive verbal or emotional attacks to anticipate perceived hostility from her spouse.
- **Purpose of Symptom:** Chronic conflict and fighting serve as a protective armor to prevent feeling deep grief, loneliness, and childhood vulnerability.

## 2. Identified Attachment Style & Core Complex
- **Attachment Style:** Autonomy Type in chronic fight-response.
- **Core Complex & Catastrophe Fantasy:**
  - *"The other person hates me, wants to destroy me, or is about to attack me."*
  - The unconscious threat representation escalates into extreme images of physical violence (e.g., *"The other person will stab me with a knife"*).
- **Suppressed Primary Emotion:** Concealed beneath the aggressive defense armor lies existential sadness, solitude, and an unmet yearning for genuine intimacy and acceptance.

## 3. Therapeutic Procedure & Key Inquiries
Direct confrontation with unconscious projections and radical sensory grounding in present-moment contact:

- **Naming the Hidden Horror Scenario:**
  Gopal insists that the client formulate her catastrophic threat image with utmost precision rather than keeping it vague, as the nervous system generates fight-flight physiology based on concrete mental images.
- **Direct Reality Testing (Reality Check):**
  - Directed inquiry to Gopal:
    - *"Gopal, do you plan to kill me right now with a knife?"*
    - **Gopal's response:** *"No, definitively not. That is simply not true."*
  - **Sensory Perception Check (Eyes & Ears):**
    - *"Do I have a knife in my hand, yes or no?"*
    - The client must look directly and verify with her own senses that no physical danger exists in the immediate environment.
- **Shift from Warfare to Emotional Vulnerability:**
  Gopal explores what happens when primary feelings replace combative defense:
  - Inquiry: *"What happens if you say: 'I feel lonely and sad'?"*
  - **Client's realization:** Stating vulnerable truth instantly fosters warmth, deep intimacy, and closeness rather than confrontation and counterattack.

## 4. Defense Mechanisms in Contact
- **Preemptive Attack (Fight):** Striking out with criticism or blame before the partner has any chance to reject or wound her.
- **Over-Responsibility & Session Management:** Worrying about exceeding time limits or burdening the therapist. Gopal exposes this as an avoidance mechanism: *"I lead this session, not you. You don't have to manage anything."*
- **Catastrophic Misinterpretation:** Minor stimuli (e.g., background noise from her daughter) are instantaneously amplified into *"Now comes catastrophe, now he will throw me out."*

## 5. Therapeutic Outcome & Insights
- **Sacrificing the Survival Mechanism for Contact:**
  - *"To experience true connection and love, we must sacrifice our old survival mechanism (war and conflict). Otherwise, genuine partnership is impossible."*
- **Definition of Authentic Contact:**
  - Contact is not about controlling the other or winning arguments, but consists of:
    1. *Honestly communicating what is happening inside oneself (feelings, needs, thoughts).*
    2. *Attentively listening to what is happening inside the other.*
- **Relief Through Disarmament:** Dropping the combative stance transforms acute nervous system tension into deep emotional presence and calm.
