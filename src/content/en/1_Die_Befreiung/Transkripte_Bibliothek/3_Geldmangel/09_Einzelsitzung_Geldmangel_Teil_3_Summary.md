# Individual Session: Money Scarcity – Part 3 (Over-Functioning, Anger & Flight from Connection)

> 📜 **Full Verbatim Transcript (approx. 41 Min.):** [Read the full transcript of this session](./Volltranskripte/09_Einzelsitzung_Geldmangel_Teil_3_Volltranskript)

## 1. Initial Situation & Symptom
The client reports pervasive, chronic scarcity across multiple areas: continuous financial struggle, lack of partnership for several years, and pervasive loneliness. Past romantic relationships repeatedly foundered on acute abandonment fear.
- **Purpose of Symptom:** Experiencing material and romantic scarcity reflects an unconscious refusal to genuinely trust a safe counterpart in the present moment.

## 2. Identified Attachment Style & Core Complex
- **Attachment Style:** Merger Type (Anxious-Preoccupied) / Over-adaptation with chronic over-functioning.
- **Core Complex (Unconscious Belief):**
  - *"I have no inherent worth. I must constantly provide added value and take care of the other person, otherwise I will be deemed useless and cast aside."*
  - When personal needs arise, immediate rejection is assumed: *"If I show needs, no one will want anything to do with me."*
- **Suppressed Rage & Sabotage:**
  Because compulsive self-denial is intolerable, substantial underlying rage and resentment accumulate. This aggression surfaces unconsciously: the client questions every gesture of goodwill, tests boundaries relentlessly, and undermines connection until the other person walks away.

## 3. Therapeutic Procedure & Key Inquiries
Gopal cuts through the client's subtle sabotage strategies with uncompromising clarity:

- **Confronting Unconscious Sabotage:**
  - *"You just abandoned me! You sacrificed me for your personal trauma drama. This is about thoughts regarding me. What you are doing is aggression — hammering the connection to create distance."*
- **Dismantling the Projection:**
  - Gopal instructs her to ask directly:
    - *"Gopal, do you plan to separate from me forever?"* $\rightarrow$ **Gopal:** *"No."*
    - *"Gopal, did you lie to me?"* $\rightarrow$ **Gopal:** *"No. I have no need to lie. I need nothing from you. There is zero problem with you."*
- **Halting Flight from Pleasant Contact:**
  When noticeable relaxation and intimacy begin to unfold in the room, her mind immediately flees into disaster scripts ("What about my parents? What if I make mistakes? What about the war in Ukraine?").
  - Gopal halts this sharply:
    - *"You are in a restaurant eating because you are hungry, and suddenly you remember global catastrophes and run out the door! Stop it. Tell your mind: No! It is pleasant right here. Leave me in peace."*

## 4. Defense Mechanisms in Contact
- **Distancing via Suspicion:** Dismissing sincere reassurance as deception or superficial calming tactics.
- **Mental Shift of Time and Location:** Escaping into the past (parents), the future, or distant crises the moment connection begins to work.
- **Fear of Making Mistakes:** Neurotic belief that failing to obey imagined orders will lead to severe punishment.

## 5. Therapeutic Outcome & Insights
- **Recognizing Adult Agency:**
  As an adult woman, she is permitted to make mistakes without consequence. The therapist issues no commands and requires nothing from her.
- **Connection Requires Dialogue Over Silence:**
  *"Silence and endurance belong to childhood. Life is reciprocal exchange. The more you communicate authentically, the less power the old trauma loop holds to drag you into catastrophe."*
- **Integration:**
  Pronounced down-regulation in autonomic arousal; the client experiences for the first time that a counterpart remains relaxed, present, and benevolent despite her skepticism and confusion.
