# Lack of money: how the merger type arises

The Merging Type develops the “lack of money” symptom from an early childhood attachment pattern. The experience of insufficient closeness is unconsciously externalized to financial issues.

## Emergence in childhood

* **Incomplete attachment:** The Merging Type experiences an insufficient or repeatedly breaking attachment to their parents in childhood.
* **Linking closeness and lack:** Closeness and relationship are inextricably linked and stored as an internal concept with the feeling of lack.
* **Compensation by caring:** As a compensatory strategy, the child begins to care more for others and develop a lot of activity to compensate for the incomplete connection.

## The unconscious mechanism in adulthood

* **Continuing the Old Program:** As an adult, the person continues this unconscious program without realizing that full and direct connections would be possible today (e.g., through honestly sharing one's truth).
* **Failing compensation:** However, the compensation strategy (taking care of things, farming, constantly reaching out to others) does not lead to the longed-for closeness.
* **Paradoxical effect:** This behavior unconsciously creates exactly the opposite: it has a distancing effect and reproduces the old inner emptiness and distance.

## Externalization to the symptom “lack of money”

* **Protection against the pain of attachment:** Since the re-experienced distance and inner emptiness are hardly bearable, the internal conflict of attachment is externalized as a protective mechanism to an external problem (e.g. lack of money).
* **The Illusion of Solvability:** Pain is lessened because the externalized problem appears as tangible and solvable (e.g., "I just need to make more money").
* **Self-sabotage:** The person is now working intensively on the topic of money. But since the actual, unconscious attachment conflict is not resolved, she repeatedly sabotages herself, thereby perpetuating the deficiency.
* **The path to the solution:** Only when the external symptom (lack of money) is traced back to the true core - the relationship level - and solved there, does the financial issue lose its existential drama and become a purely practical, easily solvable problem.
