# Stress & Burnout – Autonomy Type

Stress and burnout in the Autonomy Type are expressed by the same symptoms as in the Merging Type - excessive demands, overload and the denial of one's own feelings and needs. However, the internal configuration and the way out of this dynamic are fundamentally different.

## The internal dynamics of the Autonomy Type

* **Achievement as a raison d'être:** The Autonomy Type virtually works themselves to death on the bonding level, in the unconscious hope of being allowed to exist and to be able to come into contact at all.
* **Abstract revision:** In contrast to the Merging Type, which exhausts itself in caring for others, the Autonomy Type works through abstract constructs, thought spaces and activities.
* **Self-extinction as a strategy:** In order to be "acceptable" to others, the Autonomy Type acts as if it doesn't exist. He completely withdraws his attention from his own needs, feelings and the other person.
* **Protection from the Void:** This artificially created distance serves as a protective mechanism. The Autonomy Type protects themselves from the painful realization that (in their childhood experience) no one is there for them.

## Core beliefs and deepest emotions

* **The toxic belief:** The deepest programming of the Autonomy Type says: *“Because I exist, because I am present and have feelings and needs, no one wants to have anything to do with me.”*
* **Loneliness and sadness:** This unconscious model of life leads to complete isolation, as was often experienced in childhood. The deepest, most repelled feelings are therefore great loneliness and sadness.
* **Projection onto others:** The Autonomy Type unconsciously assumes that other people feel superior to them and completely independent, but in reality they themselves are frozen and dissociated by loneliness.
* **The turning point:** Anyone who is an autonomous type and can perceive and feel this loneliness and sadness within themselves has already taken a huge step - this corresponds to almost 50 percent of the transformation.

## The Reality Check: The Key Questions

In order to dissolve these deeply anchored beliefs, it is not enough to just treat the stress symptom. Internal beliefs about attachment and relationships need to be aligned with reality.

**Exercise for the Autonomy Type:** Ask someone close to you the following questions (reality check) to check your attachment pattern:

1. **“Do you exist, are you there?”**
2. **"Is it true that you would have a problem if I told you that I exist, that I feel lonely and sad and that I have needs?"**
3. **"Is it true that you feel somehow overpowering, oversized and completely independent of me and that you are actually dissociated and frozen inside?"**

Once these deep concepts are verbalized and tested through the other person's reactions in the current reality, the old patterns begin to fundamentally change.
