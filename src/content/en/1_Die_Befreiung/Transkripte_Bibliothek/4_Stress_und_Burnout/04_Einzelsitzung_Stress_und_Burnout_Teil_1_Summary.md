---

## 1. Initial Situation & Chronic Exhaustion
A client (mother of three children, married) has been in a state of chronic exhaustion and burnout for six years.
* **Physical Symptoms:** Chronic fatigue, dizziness, concentration difficulties, persistent "brain fog".
* **Medical Findings:** Organically healthy; no somatic pathology found.
* **Client's Own Interpretation:** Firm belief that external demands (family duties, expectations of others, societal crises) cause her stress.

---

## 2. Therapeutic Intervention: Stopping the Mental Projections
Gopal systematically interrupts the client whenever she loses herself in mental constructs, rationalizations, or reports about third parties:
* **Separation of Mind from Physical Reality:** Mental clouds concerning the world or perceived expectations are not physical input from outside.
* **Focus on the Here and Now:** Merger types constantly loop in internal narratives. The therapeutic guidance redirects them to immediate tangible reality: *What is impacting you physically RIGHT NOW in this room?*

---

## 3. The Identified Attachment Dynamic
Transitioning from the physical symptom to the relational plane reveals the core pattern:
* **Client's Complaint:** *"My partner and close people do not see me or understand me."*
* **Boundary Behavior:** She unconsciously violates others' boundaries while perpetually feeling misunderstood and isolated.
* **Childhood Pattern:** The feeling of "not being seen and understood" is the exact repetition of early childhood attachment failure.

---

## 4. The Core Mechanism: Self-Created Invisibility
Gopal clearly demonstrates how the client unconsciously stages this suffering daily:
1. **Never sharing one's own truth:** She never directly communicates her own needs, boundaries, and vulnerable feelings in contact.
2. **Compensation through caretaking:** Instead of staying grounded in herself, she constantly inquires about others and cares for their affairs (survival strategy of the merger type).
3. **Flight & withdrawal:** When emotional proximity tightens, she withdraws internally or breaks contact.
* **Outcome:** When someone hides themselves and shows nothing authentic, others **cannot** see them. The interpersonal distance is actively manufactured as a defense against real intimacy.

---

## 5. In-Depth Phase (Min. 30–52): Fight for Autonomy & The Paramedic Principle
In the second half, deep resistance against receiving help surfaces:
* **The Paramedic Metaphor:** When a paramedic arrives at a trauma scene, it is futile if the injured person says: *"I know best how to operate on myself, leave me alone!"*
* **The Unconscious Fight for Autonomy:** The client enters a combative defense: *"I am here, but I will not participate. My default answer is No."*
* **Gopal's Stance & Intervention:** Gopal does not combat her resistance, but validates it completely:
  > *"That is completely fine. That is your current level of attachment capability: stepping into contact and saying 'No, I won't cooperate, I want to be right.' Standing by that is a thousand times more healing than packaging it into a victim narrative. I have no intention whatsoever of taking away your autonomy."*
* **Breakthrough at the End:** By unconditionally allowing her fight for autonomy, her nervous system softens: the client realizes with relief that the therapist genuinely understands her without overwhelming her.

---

## 6. Key Quotes from the Session
* *"We are not in your head with your thoughts, but with physical external reality. Looking there is very hard for merger types."*
* *"You are the one generating all of this — out of nothing, without cause today. You create this entire scenario yourself."*
* *"A one-hour battle for your autonomy, which I don't oppose at all. But I never intended to strip you of independence. I only wanted to help you."*
