# Compendium: Merging Type according to Gopal

## 1. Summary of symptoms
The Merging Type shows the following typical symptoms and behavior in relationships and in conflict situations:

- **Compulsive Attraction:** A constant urge to care for, conform, and help others (helper syndrome).
- **Fear of abandonment:** Deep fear of loss and the fear of not being able to survive without the other person, which often results in feelings of loneliness, sadness and helplessness.
- **Distance through caring:** Instead of establishing real, deep contact (by sharing one's own real feelings), excessive caring and "scene pushing" (endless, irrelevant stories) unconsciously create distance.
- **Infantilization of the other person:** The partner or the other person is often viewed and treated as small, weak or helpless, similar to a small child.
- **Suppressed Autonomy:** Anger, hatred, the need for independence and freedom are strongly suppressed because these were experienced as dangerous or undesirable in childhood. An inner “crunch” or resistance to having to take care of something is an important indication of this.
- **Manipulation and control:** Emotional outbursts (e.g. drama, tears, artificial outbursts of anger) or passive-aggressive behavior (ignoring, not listening) are used unconsciously to control the other person and bind them to themselves.
- **Symptom Displacement (e.g. lack of money/burnout):** The internal lack of connection and closeness is often projected onto external areas of life. For example, an apparent problem arises with "lack of money" (a lack on the outside as a reflection of the lack of relationships) or "stress/burnout" (due to constantly sacrificing yourself for others in the hope of bonding).

## 2. Mechanism: From the symptom to the attachment level
The fundamental problem of the Merging Type lies not in the external symptoms (such as lack of money, separation or stress), but in an unconscious Attachment Trauma that is now maintained by one's own thought concepts.

**The process:**

1. **Recognize the symptom:** For example, “lack of money” (something is missing, lack) or “burnout” (overwhelm due to constant worry).
2. **Translation to the attachment level:** The lack of money is actually a perceived lack of emotional connection and closeness. The overwhelm in burnout is the constant, strenuous attempt to buy security in the relationship through adaptation.
3. **The illusion of the present:** In childhood, the distance was real and enforced by the caregivers (rejection of the child's autonomy). Today, the Merging Type creates this distance itself. He thinks he wants closeness, but through his controlling, absorbing and infantilizing behavior he creates an atmosphere from which others (especially autonomy types) instinctively flee.
4. **The Thought Construct:** The Merging Type is trapped in a mental projection. He unconsciously believes:
   - *"The other person is weak and dependent on me."*
   - *"If I don't take care of him, he'll leave me."*
   - *"If I show my true feelings (especially anger, desire for autonomy), I will be left immediately."*
5. **The role of resistance:** An important "gateway" to transformation is the subtle inner resistance against the compulsion to always have to take care of yourself. Beneath this resistance lies the actual core emotion: the suppressed anger and the desire for freedom and independence.

## 3. The solution & questioning technique (The Flow)
Healing doesn't happen through caring more or trying to change the outside (the partner). It happens through stopping taking up space, **honestly sharing** the true inner states and directly examining the unconscious projections.

**Step-by-step flow in the session:**

1. **Translate symptom to attachment:** Let the client recognize that the external symptom is a mirror for the relationship experience (lack of externality = lack of closeness).
2. **Recognize your own contribution:** Ask the client: *"How do you create this distance yourself today?"* (e.g. through infantilization, endless talking without real content, drama).
3. **Reveal protective mechanism:** *"What are you actually trying to protect yourself from by taking care of yourself?"* (Answer: From abandonment).
4. **Identify the trigger:** *"What do you think would be the absolute trigger for the other person to leave you?"* (Answer: "If I show my anger and do my own thing.")
5. **Recognize the projection:** *"How do you actually imagine the other person?"* (Answer: Small, helpless, dependent).
6. **The Reality Test (The Core Questions):** The client must check these deeply rooted concepts in reality by asking the other person (partner or therapist) direct questions.
   - *“Are you planning to leave me?”*
   - *“Do you have a problem with me telling you that I feel anger?”*
   - *“Do you have a problem with me doing my own thing and being independent?”*
   - *“Is it true that you feel small, helpless and dependent on me?”*
7. **The projection break by the other person:** An enormously powerful (often shocking) moment is when the other person or the therapist utters the sentence: *"I don't need anything from you. I don't need your care."* This immediately shatters the illusion that the bond can only be maintained through caring and throws the client back to his true feelings (sadness, anger, emptiness).
8. **Real Listening & Sharing:** The client needs to practice *really hearing* the answer (which is difficult for the Merging Type). This changes the deepest layers of the attachment system. At the same time, the client learns to honestly communicate his true feelings instead of drama: *"I'm angry," "My head is thinking you're leaving me, and I feel lonely."*
