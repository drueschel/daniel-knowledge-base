# Session 1: Overview of Developmental Milestones and Needs

In this session, Mariah Rooney and Kai Cheng Thom introduce the foundational maps and concepts necessary for navigating developmental trauma. They emphasize the importance of understanding the socio-political context of trauma, the eight sensory systems, and the psychobiological basic needs that shape early development.

## 1. The 8-Phase Map of Developmental Trauma Healing

The therapeutic journey of developmental trauma is nonlinear, but can be broadly understood through an 8-phase map. The central hub of this process is **Cultivating Somatic Awareness**. The phases typically move through:
1. **Pattern Recognition:** Noticing repetitive physical or relational shapes.
2. **Contact/Access into the Wound:** Leaning into the underlying pain.
3. **Process & Discernment (The Soup):** The messy stage of discovering what happened.
4. **Discovering Blocked Developmental Movement:** Identifying the thwarted instinct.
5. **Providing the Missing Experience:** Offering the attuned response that was lacking.
6. **Grief, Relief, Awe:** The profound emotional release that follows the missing experience.
7. **Reconstitution of Identity & Meaning Making:** Forming a new sense of self.
8. **Integration into Embodied Life & Completion:** Moving forward with new capacity.

## 2. Sensory Processing and the 8 Senses

Developmental trauma and neglect disrupt our ability to process sensory information. We have **eight** sensory systems, not just five:
- **The Traditional 5:** Touch, sight, taste, sound, smell.
- **Proprioceptive System:** The awareness of where our body is in space (muscles and joints).
- **Vestibular System:** The inner ear system that governs balance and motion.
- **Interoceptive System:** The internal tracking of bodily sensations and needs (hunger, need for the bathroom, heart rate).

When early environments lack attuned sensory input, the developing child may struggle with clumsiness (proprioception deficits), spatial disorientation, or an inability to identify their own emotions (interoception deficits). 

## 3. The Five Basic Needs

Moving beyond Maslow's hierarchy, Psychomotor Psychotherapy outlines five fundamental developmental needs, which are both literal and symbolic:
1. **Place:** Having a physical home, but also a place in a caregiver's arms and a place in their loving gaze. The feeling of belonging.
2. **Nurturance:** Receiving literal nutrients, but also sensory stimulation and emotional nourishment.
3. **Protection:** The right to be kept safe from harm by caregivers and society.
4. **Flexible Loving Limits:** Boundaries that provide containment and safety without being rigid, harsh, or punitive.
5. **Support:** Receiving the "just right" amount of physical and emotional backing to grow.

## 4. Expanding the Map of the Nervous System

While trauma work often focuses on survival states, healthy development also requires states of arousal and pleasure:
- **Awake and Take (Sympathetic Dominant):** The state of excitement, play, and ecstasy. Children need this rhythm of excitation to learn how to engage dynamically with the world. 
- **Rest and Digest (Parasympathetic Dominant):** The state of blissful relaxation and peace. The ability to let go completely without vigilance.

In developmental trauma, these states of pleasure and rest are often forbidden or inaccessible, leaving the individual trapped in either hypervigilance (Fight/Flight) or numbed disconnection (Freeze/Appease). True regulation involves expanding the window of capacity to safely experience joy and bliss.
