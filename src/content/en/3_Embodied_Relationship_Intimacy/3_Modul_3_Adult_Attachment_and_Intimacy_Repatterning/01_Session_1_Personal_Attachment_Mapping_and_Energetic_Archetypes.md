# Session 1: Personal Attachment Mapping – Somatic Attachment Archetypes & Re-Patterning

This session opens Module 3 (*Adult Attachment and Intimacy Re-Patterning*) of the *Embodied Intimacy & Relationship Coaching* program. Facilitated by **Dr. Scott Lyons** (Licensed Clinical Psychologist, Doctor of Osteopathy, Mind-Body Medicine specialist, founder of *The Embody Lab*, and author of *Addicted to Drama*), the lesson initiates a radical paradigm shift: moving away from the pathologizing, shame-laden labels of classical psychology toward a compassionate, somatic, and strength-based understanding of attachment.

Attachment patterns are not framed here as static deficits or personality disorders, but as **brilliant somatic survival and protective adaptations** developed by the nervous system. The session's core guiding principle is:  
> **"We are not our patterns. We are not our survival strategies."**  
> *(They are adaptive strategies our organism learned to navigate stress and lack of safety. What was learned can be somatically re-patterned, expanded, and healed.)*

## 1. Re-Envisioning Attachment: De-Shaming & Somatic Wholeness

### 1.1 Critique of Traditional Attachment Research
Dr. Scott Lyons deconstructs the historical and cultural limitations of classical attachment theory (Bowlby, Ainsworth):
1. **The "Mother-Blaming" Trap:** Historically, attachment styles were almost exclusively attributed to maternal behavior, ignoring fathers, non-binary caregivers, siblings, co-parents, and the surrounding social environment.
2. **Narrow Focus on the Western Nuclear Family:** In many cultures, child-rearing is communal (*Alloparenting*). A model evaluating only an isolated primary caregiver fails to reflect evolutionary and cross-cultural realities.
3. **Neglect of Structural Harm and Systemic Inequity:** Bonding disruptions often stem not from a lack of parental love, but from socioeconomic deprivation, systemic racism, resource scarcity, housing instability, medical trauma, and caregiver exhaustion.
4. **The "60% Secure Attachment" Myth:** Classical research claims approximately 60% of people have secure attachment. Dr. Lyons challenges this: researchers simply lacked nuanced somatic observation tools. Individuals who did not fit the three crude categories (*anxious*, *avoidant*, *disorganized*) were defaulted into "secure."
5. **The Illusion of Permanence:** Attachment is not a fixed, lifelong stamp. A secure attachment can erode through abusive relationships or traumatic life events; conversely, adult relational security can be built and anchored through somatic re-patterning (*Earned Secure Attachment*).

### 1.3 The Developmental Arc: From Co-Regulation to Self-Regulation
Human infants lack the neurobiological architecture for self-regulation. When confronted with cold, hunger, sensory overwhelm, or isolation, their nervous system spirals into distress:
- **Caregivers as a Surrogate Nervous System:** The primary caregiver loans their own regulated nervous system to the baby (*Surrogate Nervous System*), acting as an external emotional container.
- **The Primacy of Touch:** Touch and movement are the earliest developmental senses through which bonding and felt safety are communicated.
- **The Genesis of Survival Patterns:** When reliable co-regulation is missing, the infant's organism must develop compensatory armoring and adaptations—which become adult attachment stances.

### 2.1 Practice 1: Grounding & Anchoring
*Duration: ~15–20 minutes. Posture: Seated, lying down, or standing.*
1. **The Axial Gravity Waterfall:**
   - Soften the gaze or close eyes. Direct awareness to the crown and upper cervical spine. Introduce micro-movements.
   - Sense the present weight (not collapsed or inert, but alive and responsive) pouring like a warm waterfall from the neck into the thoracic spine.
   - Continue cascading down into the lumbar spine, sacrum, and coccyx. The spine becomes a sturdy central mooring post.
   - Allow weight to pour through the limbs: shoulders, arms, hands; pelvis, thighs, shins, down to the soles of the feet.
2. **Porous Roots into the Earth:**
   - Envision roots extending from the feet and tailbone (or the entire back body if lying down), penetrating floorboards and concrete effortlessly.
   - Roots burrow into the dark, damp, fertile earth below.
   - *Cultivating Porosity:* The root walls become porous, drawing up the earth's limitless vitality like liquid nourishment into feet, pelvis, spine, and head.
3. **Yielding (Sinking into the Ground):**
   - Release resistance into gravity (*Yielding*). Notice any inner restlessness or distrust not as failure, but as vital information: *"A part of me feels cautious. I invite that part to be present here."*
4. **3D Spatial Orientation while Maintaining Anchor:**
   - Gently open eyes. Take in ambient temperature, scents, room lighting, and sounds. Shift weight subtly side-to-side and front-to-back, anchoring 100% into the earth while maintaining perceptual contact with the room.

### 2.2 Practice 2: Embodied Containment
Without a defined physical container, emotions either blast outward destructively or collapse into internal overwhelm. Containment is not suppression; it is providing a safe, resilient holding space.
1. **Tactile Topography Mapping (External Container):**
   - Using palms and fingertips with firm proprioceptive pressure, trace and map the outer contours of the entire body: skull, neck, shoulders, ribcage, abdomen, pelvic bowl, hips, legs, and feet.
   - The nervous system receives the sensory message: *"Here is where I begin, and here is where my body ends."*
2. **Full-Body Condensation & Expansion:**
   - Compress the entire organism 3-dimensionally inward toward the core (centripetal hug from crown to toes without holding the breath).
   - Expand back outward, but only as far as boundary clarity can be tracked (avoiding dissipation into numbness or fuzziness).
3. **Mapping the Inner Lining & Back Body (Internal Container):**
   - Place one hand on the sternum (front) and one hand on the thoracic spine (back).
   - Use breath to trace the interior walls of the vessel. Pay specific attention to the *Back Body* (behind the heart and kidneys), an area frequently neglected in forward-leaning cultures.

### 2.3 Practice 3: Filling & Presence
An empty vessel invites intrusive foreign energies or collapses into loneliness.
1. **Magnetic Gathering of Scattered Energy:**
   - Recall attention, vitality, and psychic energy scattered across past relationships, unresolved arguments, family expectations, or work stress back into the container like iron filings to a magnet.
2. **The Resonance of One's Name:**
   - Speak your birth or chosen name silently within. Locate where that name vibrates in the physical body (chest, throat, solar plexus, pelvis) and observe its unique somatic texture.
3. **Energetic Clearing (Vacuuming / Mailing Back):**
   - Bring to mind a person currently occupying significant internal mental/emotional space (ex-partner, demanding client, parent).
   - Sense where their presence is lodged inside your container.
   - Somatically vacuum or gather that foreign energy, place it in an imaginary envelope, and mail it back to its rightful owner with dignity and clarity (*"This is yours, not mine"*).
   - Immediately refill the freed space with your own core essence and breath.
4. **The Relational Stress Test:**
   - Place an intimate relational partner or significant figure in your imagined space.
   - Track instantaneous somatic changes: *Does grounding collapse? Does breath freeze? Does energy puncture forward, or pull inward into hiding?*

## 3. The Spectrum of 11+ Somatic & Energetic Archetypes

### 3.1 Detailed Archetype Breakdown

#### 1. LEAVE (Dissociation / Evacuation)
- **Somatic Vector:** Consciousness and energetic charge ascend upward through the crown into space.
- **Physical Presentation:** Shallow apical breathing, glassy unanchored gaze, cold feet, feeling floaty or observing oneself from above.
- **Internal Narrative:** *"I have to get out of here. It is too terrifying to inhabit this body."*
- **Etiology:** Extreme early overwhelm, severe medical trauma, or existential developmental threat.

#### 2. HYPER-CONNECT (Fawning / People-Pleasing / Self-Abandonment)
- **Somatic Vector:** Attention leaps out of the personal vessel to track and accommodate the other.
- **Physical Presentation:** Fixed appeasing smile, chronic head-nodding, forward posture, internal emotional collapse concealed by hyper-competence.
- **Internal Narrative:** *"If I can anticipate your needs and keep you pleased, you will not leave me."*
- **Etiology:** Unpredictable, depressed, or volatile caregivers who rewarded self-erasure.

#### 3. HIDE (Emergency Braking / Overriding)
- **Somatic Vector:** The organism slams on the full-body physiological brakes.
- **Physical Presentation:** Hunched shoulders, compressed ribcage, teeth-clenching, enduring silently. Emotions are suppressed until leaking as passive-aggressive hostility or sudden eruptions.
- **Internal Narrative:** *"I will make myself invisible and tolerate this until the danger passes."*
- **Etiology:** Chaotic, loud, or authoritarian homes where emotional self-expression was punished.

#### 4. RETRACTING / SHRINKING (Core Withdrawal)
- **Somatic Vector:** The core pulls inward toward a microscopic point away from the periphery.
- **Physical Presentation:** Unlike *Hide* (which bears down), *Retracting* pulls tissues inward from the contact boundary, creating an armored shell while the internal self shrinks.
- **Internal Narrative:** *"Do not touch me. I am entirely alone inside here."*
- **Etiology:** Boundary-violating, enmeshing, or invasive caregivers who obliterated privacy.

#### 5. FIGHTING (Confrontational Puncture)
- **Somatic Vector:** The front boundary is punctured; energy blasts forward aggressively.
- **Physical Presentation:** Thrust chin, narrowed pupils, clenched fists, expanded chest, disconnection from back body and feet.
- **Internal Narrative:** *"Attack is the best defense. Before you can hurt me, I will conquer you."*
- **Etiology:** Environments where vulnerability was mocked or dangerous, and aggression was required for safety.

#### 6. RIGID (Impenetrability / Cold Armor)
- **Somatic Vector:** Total fascial and muscular freeze.
- **Physical Presentation:** Stiff spine, wooden movements, flat vocal affect, formal posture. Inability to access softness or fluid surrender.
- **Internal Narrative:** *"I am indestructible. If nothing reaches me, nothing can break me."*
- **Etiology:** High-pressure performance environments, puritanical moralism, or strict emotional suppression.

#### 7. CHASING / OVER-EXTENDING / GRASPING
- **Somatic Vector:** Falling forward out of one's center of gravity.
- **Physical Presentation:** Reaching hands, strained neck, heels lifting off the floor, panting breath.
- **Internal Narrative:** *"Don't pull away! Look at me! Tell me I matter!"*
- **Etiology:** Chronic emotional unavailability and erratic affection from early caregivers.

#### 8. MERGING (Enmeshment / Symbiosis)
- **Somatic Vector:** Dissolution of the skin boundary between self and other.
- **Physical Presentation:** The other person is used as one's sole anchor. Internal bodily signals (hunger, exhaustion, libido) are blurred with partner states.
- **Internal Narrative:** *"Without you, I do not exist. We are one entity."*
- **Etiology:** Parentification, where the child served as an emotional surrogate partner for a parent.

#### 9. FIXATION (Externalized Relational Obsession)
- **Somatic Vector:** High-intensity spotlight focused entirely on the partner's actions and flaws.
- **Physical Presentation:** Fixed staring, strained cervical spine, neglected respiration. In coaching, spends the session analyzing the partner.
- **Internal Narrative:** *"If they would just change this one thing, our relationship would be saved."*
- **Etiology:** A defensive distraction strategy designed to avoid feeling one's own core abandonment agony.

#### 10. SPILLING / LEAKING / COLLAPSING
- **Somatic Vector:** Complete rupture and loss of vessel walls.
- **Physical Presentation:** Floppy muscle tone, uncontained emotional crying, helpless childlike demeanor, expectation that others must soothe them.
- **Internal Narrative:** *"I am helpless. You must fix this for me."*
- **Etiology:** Inconsistent over-protection paired with suppression of autonomous coping agency.

#### 11. LOGICAL (Intellectualization / Somatic Severance)
- **Somatic Vector:** Consciousness retreats entirely into the neocortex.
- **Physical Presentation:** Disconnection at the throat. Eloquent, cerebral articulation about feelings with zero visceral connection below the neck.
- **Internal Narrative:** *"Emotions are irrational and messy. If I analyze everything, I remain safe."*
- **Etiology:** Upbringing where emotional vulnerability was stigmatized as weakness and intellect was prized.

#### 12. PERFECTIONISM (Anxiety Micro-Management)
- **Somatic Vector:** Rigorous vigilance over all somatic, behavioral, and relational expressions.
- **Physical Presentation:** Chronic tension in jaw, upper neck, and fingers; inability to relax into spontaneity.
- **Internal Narrative:** *"If I can execute everything without flaw, ruptures can never happen."*
- **Etiology:** Affection was strictly conditional on flawless performance.

## 5. Relational Blueprints Practice

A 3-part structured assessment process for mapping developmental relationship blueprints:

### 5.1 The Safety & Love Triad
Clients explore and articulate:
1. **What fosters felt safety and love from within myself?** (e.g., grounding routines, clear limits, solitary downtime, self-touch)
2. **What fosters felt safety and love from others?** (e.g., calm vocal tone, reliability, non-demanding touch, attunement)
3. **What fosters felt safety and love from my environment?** (e.g., nature, gravity, dim lighting, organized clean spaces)

### 5.2 Family Genogram Mapping
- Sketch the relational dynamics of the family of origin (parents, grandparents, siblings).
- *Inquiry:* What implicit somatic beliefs about intimacy, vulnerability, conflict, and autonomy did I absorb from this field?

### 5.3 Seven Relationship Audit Questions
For any significant past or present relationship:
```
1. What worked? What behaviors and qualities did I genuinely cherish?
2. What did not work? What felt intolerable or violating?
3. What mistakes did my partner make? What mistakes did I make?
4. What is my real-time somatic response when attuning to that person today?
5. What is my real-time somatic response when attuning to the relationship dynamic as a whole?
6. Which energetic archetype / survival strategy dominated this partnership?
7. What resilient skills, wisdom, and boundary clarity did I gain from this relationship?
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Strength-Based Reframe:**  
> Every single attachment archetype preserved our life and cultivated real superpowers. The *Hyper-Connector* possesses profound empathy; the *Logical* possesses sharp analytical discernment; the *Fighter* possesses fierce boundary courage; the *Leave* archetype possesses deep imaginative and spiritual depth. Healing is not eradicating the strategy, but restoring **conscious somatic choice** over automatic compulsion.

## 7. Key Takeaways for Intimacy Coaches

1. **De-Pathologize:** Attachment styles are somatic survival adaptations, not broken identities.
2. **Body Before Mind:** Without grounding, containment, and presence, cognitive insight cannot alter nervous system reactivity.
3. **Multidimensional Attachment:** Always restore the client's relationship with their own body and gravity before addressing interpersonal conflict.
4. **Phenomenological Precision:** Utilize the 11+ bioenergetic archetypes to track what is happening underneath verbal narratives.
5. **Earned Security:** Attachment is neuroplastically rewirable. Attuned, co-regulated coaching sessions literally rewire the client's blueprint for secure intimacy.
