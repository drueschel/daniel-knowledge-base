# Inner disorientation

This text deals with the initial inner disorientation on the path to overcoming trauma and self-awareness. It is emphasized that the difficulty in distinguishing between thoughts, feelings and physical sensations is completely normal and is not an obstacle, but is the natural beginning of the healing path.

## Normality of ambiguity
- At the beginning it is completely normal to be internally disorientated and not be able to clearly distinguish between thoughts, feelings or physical sensations.
- In contrast to other traditions (such as in Tibet), in our culture we are not trained to clearly differentiate between these levels.
- The main purpose of therapeutic work is precisely to gradually build this clarity.

## Dealing with disorientation (honest sharing with yourself)
- The initial ambiguity is neither a problem nor an obstacle, but simply the starting point for the work.
- **The most important practice:** The simplest and most effective thing is to first honestly admit your own disorientation.
- **Examples of this inner attitude:** "I'm disoriented right now" (thought level) or "I don't know which level is active right now, whether it's a feeling or not."
- Instead of frantically searching for clarity or trying to force it, the current ambiguity should be radically accepted.

## Development of clarity
- Clarity is a process that takes time and patience.
- The more regularly your attention is directed inwards, the more differentiated and clear your perception will become over time.
