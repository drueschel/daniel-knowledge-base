# What is danger and what is not

This text addresses the fundamental difference between real, physical danger to life and the subjective feeling of threat triggered by Developmental Trauma. It explains why your own nervous system sends false alarms in unresolved traumatic states and how you can use your physical senses to find a reliable connection to objective reality again.

## The deception of the nervous system in Developmental Trauma
- In the case of Developmental Trauma, the organism often signals extreme threat, fear or panic, even though there is no real danger outside.
- This is a re-enactment of old conflicts. The nervous system “lies” to us in these moments.
- As long as the trauma is not transformed, the body's signals and subtle perceptions ("fine antennas") cannot be trusted.

## Definition of a real danger
- A danger is **always** an immediate threat to physical life, survival, or physical health in the here and now (e.g., a burning house, an armed attacker, acute hunger).
- A real physical danger always requires an immediate need for action (fight, flight, etc.), for which the body provides energy.

## Emotional “danger” in adults
- Things that feel emotionally dangerous or hurtful do not pose a mortal danger to adults.
- While emotional threats massively disrupt a child's development, adult development is already complete. At this level, physical survival can no longer be threatened.

## The objective senses as the only orientation
- In order to realistically assess a situation, we must not rely on inner feelings or subtle perceptions.
- The only reliable measure is the external physical senses: **vision and hearing**.
- Only by objectively viewing and listening to the environment in the here and now ("looking out and listening") can it be determined without a doubt whether there is actually a need for action.
