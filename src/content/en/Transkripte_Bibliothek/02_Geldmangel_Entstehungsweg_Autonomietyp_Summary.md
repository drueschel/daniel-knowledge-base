# Lack of money: path to emergence of Autonomy Type

The Autonomy Type develops the symptom “lack of money” from an early childhood attachment pattern. The experience of emotional neglect leads to a fundamental existential struggle that is unconsciously projected onto financial issues.

## Emergence in childhood

* **Lack of attachment experience:** The Autonomy Type develops from an environment in which significantly less attachment and nurturing contact is experienced than with the Merging Type.
* **Emotional neglect & boundary crossing:** Parents completely neglect the child emotionally or behave in a boundary-crossing manner.
* **Resignation and withdrawal:** The child completely gives up trying to establish a connection. He realizes that no positive relationship experience is possible in this environment, switches off internally and withdraws into himself.
* **The illusion of impossibility:** The Autonomy Type grows up with the deep belief that closeness and love are not possible ("There is no one").
* **Existential distress:** Since a child absolutely needs a nurturing, mirroring counterpart in order to survive, this deficiency creates a severe, existential distress in the nervous system. This experience is often more dramatic and traumatic than the Merging Type.

## The unconscious mechanism in adulthood

* **Questioning one's own existence:** Even in adulthood, the autonomous type unconsciously experiences a constant questioning of their existence ("Am I allowed to live? Do I even exist?").
* **Failing compensation:** He tries to secure his right to exist through abstract performance and mental spaces, but true connection does not emerge that way.
* **Paradoxical effect:** The lack of money is unconsciously self-created. The person affected works through it and tries desperately to solve the problem externally, but at the same time torpedoes his own attempts at a solution.

## Externalization to the symptom “lack of money”

* **Protection from the pain of existence:** The internal, unbearable existential threat is outsourced (externalized) to the topic of money.
* **Difference from the Merging Type:** While the Merging Type is concerned with “getting enough” when it comes to money, the Autonomy Type tends to experience a lack of money as an *existential threat*.
* **Self-sabotage:** By maintaining the symptom (the lack of money), the person remains seemingly capable of acting on the outside, while the actual pain - the lack of nourishing connection - remains repressed.
* **The path to the solution:** It's not about the symptom of "lack of money" but about the ability to create real, nourishing relationships today.
