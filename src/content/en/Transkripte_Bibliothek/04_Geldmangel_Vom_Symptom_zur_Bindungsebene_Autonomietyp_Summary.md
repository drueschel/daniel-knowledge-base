# Lack of money: From the symptom to the level of attachment – ​​Autonomy Type

The Autonomy Type creates the “lack of money” symptom unconsciously at the attachment level. The basic principle behind the lack of money is the loss or non-existence, which in the Autonomy Type transfers to fundamental existential relationship questions.

## From symptom to basic principle

* **The core pattern:** Behind the symptom of lack of money is the basic principle of lack, loss and non-existence.
* **Translation to the attachment level:** On the relationship level, for the Autonomy Type it is fundamentally about their own **existence**. The unconscious core question is: “Is there even a counterpart so that I can exist?”

## The unconscious mechanism: distance through self-extinction

In order to re-enact the familiar childish feeling that “no one is there,” the autonomous type unconsciously makes themselves invisible:

* **Self-extinction as a strategy:** The Autonomy Type acts as if it doesn't even exist. He ignores his own feelings and needs.
* **Escape into the abstract:** Specifically, this happens by completely withdrawing the focus of attention from yourself and others and directing it into abstract, mental or spiritual spaces.
* **Distance as protection:** This isolation serves to create distance in order to protect oneself from the overwhelming experience and the existential distress (frozenness, dissociation) that no one could actually be there.

## Suppressed emotions and projections

* **Their own existence as a threat:** The Autonomy Type believes deep down that their existence alone is the reason other people disappear or break off contact.
* **Paradoxical attempts at contact:** He tries to establish contact by completely withdrawing himself and making himself invisible - a paradoxical mechanism that inevitably leads to deep loneliness and sadness.
* **The image of the other:** The Autonomy Type often sees the other person as apparently overpowering and independent, but unconsciously assumes that the other person is internally frozen and dissociated and would collapse in the event of real emotional contact.
* **The maximum trigger:** There is a deep conviction that if he shows himself authentically and says: "I long for contact, I exist, I have needs" it will lead to catastrophe.

## The Reality Check: The practical exercise

In order to dissolve these deeply rooted beliefs, one's inner beliefs about attachment and relationships must be aligned with reality. Today, the mental construct of constant threat usually only exists in one's own head.

**Exercise for the Autonomy Type:** Ask someone close to you (e.g. partner or therapist) the following questions directly. The important thing is to be open and willing to really hear and absorb the answer:

1. **"Are you there? Are you there?"**
2. **"Is it true that you would have a problem if I told you that I feel lonely and sad and that I exist?"**
3. **"Is it true that you feel overwhelmingly powerful and completely independent of me and are actually frozen and dissociated inside?"**

> **Note on implementation:** If the system cannot record the answers directly, the questions will be carefully repeated until clarity is achieved in depth.
