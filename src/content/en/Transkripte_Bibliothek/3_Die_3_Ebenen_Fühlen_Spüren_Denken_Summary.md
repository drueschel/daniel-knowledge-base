# The 3 levels: feeling, sensing, thinking

At the beginning of the inner path, one's own self-perception is often diffuse and unclear. Many people feel disoriented and insecure when it comes to naming and classifying internal processes. This is a completely normal condition, as our society suppresses rather than promotes contact with the inner world. However, with patience and practice, a clear differentiation of the three inner levels – physical sensations, thoughts and feelings – can be learned.

## The three levels of perception

In order to bring structure to the inner ambiguity, we can orientate ourselves on certain "starting points" that are always present:

*   **Sensing (body level):** The perception of the physical body is always available. We can sense at any time whether we are warm or cold, or whether areas feel tense or relaxed.
*   **Thinking (mental level):** The mind is constantly active. Insecurity is also often expressed in thoughts such as *"I don't know what that is"*, *"Do I feel anything at all?"* ​​or *"I'm disoriented"*.
*   **Feeling (emotional level):** Accessing real emotions is initially the most difficult thing for many people. Repressing feelings was a protective mechanism to avoid being constantly confronted with everything.

## Dealing with inner ambiguity

*   **Patience and caution:** It is essential not to put pressure on yourself. Handle your inner life slowly and carefully.
*   **No forcing clarity:** Don't try to force clarity. It's completely okay to admit your current state: *"I don't know now, I'm uncertain."* (Whereby "security/uncertainty" often points to the emotional level, while the determination of this takes place at the thought level).
*   **Continuous Practice:** Through careful, repeated looking, natural clarity develops over time.

**The goal:**
With practice you will be able to separate the layers cleanly. You will be able to accurately distinguish whether it is a physical sensation, a real feeling or a thought - and above all, recognize whether you are really feeling something or just *think* you are feeling something. Then the initial disorientation disappears.
