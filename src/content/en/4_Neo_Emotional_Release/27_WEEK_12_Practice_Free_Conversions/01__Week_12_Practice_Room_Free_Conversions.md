# ► Week 12 Practice Room | Free Conversions

## Key Concepts
- The basic steps of logging in, finding a problem, defining an emotion, and converting it to a part remain the same as previous parts work.
- The core focus shifts to understanding the part's role, intention, and function within your psyche, assuming that most parts are trying to do something beneficial for you.
- By uncovering a part's age, origin, and deepest fears or needs, you can offer it the support it requires to let go of problematic behaviors.
- The interaction builds a relationship where the adult self acknowledges the part's hard work, expresses appreciation, and meets its underlying needs.

## Summary
In this second iteration of the parts work exercise, the foundational steps of somatic integration and converting an emotion into a part remain unchanged. However, the intention behind interacting with the part is entirely different. Instead of focusing on unexpressed feelings between the individual and the part, the goal is to understand the part's active role, intentions, and the protective functions it performs in the system. The underlying assumption is that parts, even those creating problems, have positive intentions and are usually trying to keep you safe.

To facilitate this understanding, the questions for the part delve into its age, origins, fears, and needs. By asking what the part is trying to achieve and what it needs from the adult self, the practitioner helps uncover the deeper reasons behind its behaviors. This process allows the practice partner to see the part's hard work and express genuine appreciation and gratitude for its efforts. 

Ultimately, this exercise aims to foster a supportive internal relationship. As the practice partner steps into the role of the capable adult who can meet the part's needs, the part naturally feels safer. Once its needs are met and its fears are addressed, the part is often much more willing to drop the problematic strategies and behaviors it previously relied on.
