# 10 Sadness

## Core Statements
- **An Inward-Directed Emotion:** Sadness is an inward-destabilizing emotion. In order to feel sadness or grief, we must be in deep touch with ourselves.
- **Reaction to Loss:** Sadness plays a massive role whenever our soul feels like it is losing a part of itself. This loss could be a connection to another person or an aspect of our own reality.
- **The Reintegration (Connection):** Sadness is the feeling of the movement between separation and connection. For instance, when a movie "moves" you to tears, you are experiencing sadness because something is coming together and reintegrating within you.
- **The Friction of Falling Apart:** Conversely, we also feel sadness when things fall apart. The friction between two parts of yourself (or between you and someone else) that you identify with and desire connection with, is experienced as sadness.

## Transcript Summary
In this very brief segment, sadness is defined not merely as "feeling bad," but as an energetic movement between connection and separation.

Sadness is strongly directed inward and can destabilize our ego. It occurs when we lose something that helped define our identity—whether that is a loved one or a piece of our lived reality. 

At the same time, sadness is the emotion of reintegration. The tears we shed during a touching moment are the physical manifestation of separate parts within us finding each other again. Sadness is the palpable friction created when deep connections either break apart or heal back together.

## Theses
1. Sadness is the emotional expression of the soul perceiving that a part of itself is splitting off or being lost.
2. Crying out of being "touched" is an act of sadness: it is the movement of reintegration, where separated aspects are brought back together.
