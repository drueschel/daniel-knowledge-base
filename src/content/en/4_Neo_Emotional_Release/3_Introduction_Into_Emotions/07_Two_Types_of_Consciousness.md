# 07 Two Types of Consciousness

## Core Statements
- **Two Types of People:** Generally, people fall into two categories based on how they stabilize themselves emotionally.
  - Some stabilize through **outward** emotions (e.g., anger).
  - Others stabilize through **inward** emotions (e.g., sadness, fear).
- **Victim Consciousness (Inward Emotions):** People who are stuck ("looping") in sadness or fear have trouble connecting with their own personal power, strength, and anger. They remain trapped in the victim role, feeling weak, and often blame themselves.
- **Perpetrator Consciousness (Outward Emotions):** On the other side of the map is the perpetrator consciousness. Here, people constantly project their energy outward. Instead of reflecting on their own faults, they blame others, attack others, and are unable to see their own role in their suffering.
- **The Goal of the Work:** Being locked in either the left (inward) or the right (outward) extreme leads nowhere. The goal of Emotional Release is to naturally balance these qualities by allowing the incomplete emotional processes to finish.

## Transcript Summary
In this very brief video, a simple yet profound concept is introduced: how humans use emotions to stay stuck in familiar patterns.

The speaker explains that we tend to "loop" (get stuck) in one of two extremes: Victim Consciousness or Perpetrator Consciousness. Those trapped in Victim Consciousness direct their emotions (like fear and sadness) inward. They feel inherently weak and have lost touch with their natural, protective aggression and anger.

The exact opposite is true for Perpetrator Consciousness. Here, individuals direct their energy outward, often in the form of anger. They seek the cause of their suffering externally, constantly attacking or blaming others because they are unable or unwilling to engage in self-reflection. True healing means balancing this pendulum by allowing ourselves to feel the suppressed emotions (the feared opposite pole), thereby letting the body complete its natural emotional cycle.

## Theses
1. Emotional stagnation ("looping") occurs when we rely entirely on either inward-directed emotions (victimhood) or outward-directed emotions (perpetrator stance).
2. Healing requires breaking out of one's preferred polarity—the victim must access their anger and power, while the perpetrator must access their sadness and take responsibility for their own suffering.
