# 08 Emotions Map: Anger & Fear

## Core Statements
- **Two Directions:** On the "Emotions Map," emotions are fundamentally divided into two directions:
  - **Outward stabilizing emotions** (e.g., anger)
  - **Inward destabilizing emotions** (e.g., fear, sadness)
- **The Function of Anger:**
  - Anger is a fiery, strong, and outward-directed emotion.
  - It draws our attention away from our own vulnerability and directs it toward the outside world.
  - Function: It serves to protect our territory, fight for our survival, protect the people we love, and establish clear boundaries.
- **Repressed Anger vs. Cholerics:**
  - Individuals with a lot of repressed anger are often hyper-empathic, overly kind ("nice guy" syndrome), and have very poor boundaries. They do not allow themselves the space to witness or feel anger.
  - Cholerics (people who are constantly angry) do *not* actually have an anger issue; they have a *vulnerability issue*. They "loop" in anger because it prevents them from having to feel their softer, inward-directed emotions (like sadness or fear).
- **The Function of Fear:**
  - Fear sits on the exact opposite side of the map from anger. It stabilizes us inward, but it destabilizes our sense of self.
  - We feel fragile, vulnerable, and become hyper-aware of external threats. 
  - Fear (flight) and anger (fight) represent the two basic survival reactions of the human nervous system.

## Transcript Summary
In this lesson, the two core emotions of anger and fear are juxtaposed, illustrating how they tie directly into the "fight or flight" survival responses.

The focus is first placed on anger. Anger is a strongly outward-directed energy whose primary function is to set boundaries and defend one's territory. Interestingly, people who naturally lean toward being empathic, nice, and kind often harbor massive amounts of repressed anger because they have culturally or personally deemed the emotion unacceptable.

Conversely, choleric individuals use anger as a shield. They get stuck looping in anger, not because they have too much of it, but because the outward focus of anger protects them from feeling their inner vulnerability, grief, or fear.

Fear acts as the exact opposite pole to anger. It draws attention inward, makes the sense of self feel fragile, and makes us acutely sensitive to external threats. Together, these two emotions form the absolute foundation of our emotional survival strategies.

## Theses
1. Repressed anger is almost always paired with a lack of boundaries and a hyper-empathic, overly accommodating personality.
2. A choleric person doesn't have an anger problem; rather, they have a profound fear of feeling their own vulnerability (sadness, fear).
