# Emotional Regulation

This chapter explores the critical role of the autonomic nervous system and emotional regulation in our overall mental health. By understanding how the body and mind interact through the nervous system, we can see why an emotion-centric, bottom-up approach is often more effective than traditional top-down methods.

## Top-Down vs. Bottom-Up Therapy
- **Top-Down Therapy:** Traditional approaches like cognitive behavioral therapy or talk therapy start with the mind and try to work down to the body. It relies on trying to fix thoughts in the head first, often leaving the body out of the process.
- **Bottom-Up Therapy:** A somatic approach that begins with the body's sensory experience. Because 80% of the vagus nerve's fibers send signals from the body to the brain (afferent pathways), and only 20% go from the brain to the body (efferent pathways), the body informs the mind far more than the other way around. In essence, we are "80% feeling and 20% thinking."

## Mental Health is Affect Health
- The emotional data running on the nervous system's pathways is known as **affect**. 
- Because the vast majority of information flows from the body to the brain, mental health is fundamentally affect health, or emotional health.
- Addressing emotions directly is like using a large lever to help someone out of a heavy state, rather than using the small tool of mental-only approaches.

## The Neuro-Sequential Model: Regulate, Relate, Reason
Dr. Bruce Perry's model demonstrates the hierarchy of human connection and cognitive processing:
1. **Regulate:** Establish a calm, centered state in the nervous system.
2. **Relate:** Connect with the other person emotionally and socially.
3. **Reason:** Engage the prefrontal cortex for logic and problem-solving.

You cannot reason with someone who is depressed, angry, or triggered. The older, survival-focused centers of the brain inhibit the higher thinking centers when the body is dysregulated.

## Emotional Dysregulation
- **Dysregulation** is the inability to effectively manage, respond to, or diffuse uncomfortable emotions.
- When dysregulated, you become overwhelmed, lose access to your intuition and higher intelligence, and struggle to navigate situations.
- Interacting from a dysregulated state often leads to drama, arguments, isolation, and poor decision-making. The root cause of most chronic dysregulation is unresolved trauma.

## Signs of Healing and Better Regulation
- Emotional regulation is as fundamental to self-care as brushing your teeth.
- You know you are healing and becoming more regulated when:
  - The intensity of emotional triggers decreases.
  - You are triggered less frequently.
  - The duration of the trigger is significantly shorter.
