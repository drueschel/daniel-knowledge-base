# Two Types of Consciousness

In the Neo-Emotional Release methodology, emotional processing often reveals two distinct patterns of stabilization: inward-focused and outward-focused emotions. Recognizing whether a person defaults to an inward "victim" or an outward "perpetrator" consciousness is crucial for guiding them toward balance and allowing the emotional process to naturally reach completion.

## Inward Emotions: Victim Consciousness

When individuals stabilize on inward emotions such as sadness and fear, they often experience:
- **Disconnection from Power:** Difficulty accessing personal power, strength, might, and healthy anger.
- **Emotional Looping:** Getting stuck in repetitive programming that reinforces feelings of weakness or helplessness.
- **Victim Mentality:** Continuously experiencing life from a place of victimization, unable to step into their own authority.

## Outward Emotions: Perpetrator Consciousness

Conversely, stabilizing on outward emotions leads to a different set of challenges:
- **External Blame:** A tendency to constantly blame and attack others rather than taking personal responsibility.
- **Lack of Self-Reflection:** An inability to reflect on one's own faults or acknowledge the role they play in their own suffering.
- **Perpetrator Dynamics:** Engaging in behaviors that project unresolved pain onto others to avoid inward reflection.

## The Goal of Emotional Processing

Being locked in either extreme prevents true healing and personal growth. In this therapeutic work, the ultimate goal is to:
- **Restore Balance:** Naturally harmonize these opposing emotional qualities.
- **Complete the Cycle:** Facilitate a safe space where the emotional process is allowed to fully finish, freeing the individual from these restrictive consciousness loops.
