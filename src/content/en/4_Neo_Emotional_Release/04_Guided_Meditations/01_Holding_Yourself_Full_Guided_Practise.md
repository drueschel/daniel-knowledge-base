# Holding Yourself: A Guided Practice

This guided meditation practice is designed to help you access your inner space of awareness and foster a "master state." By allowing yourself to be exactly as you are without trying to achieve a specific mental state, you cultivate true presence and self-acceptance.

## Body Scan and Grounding
- Start by finding a comfortable position, allowing your spine and legs to support your body's weight.
- Bring awareness systematically through the body: feet, legs, hips, spine, belly, chest, arms, throat, and head.
- Grounding your awareness in the physical body prepares you for deeper self-presence.

## The Art of Allowing
- Recognize that meditation does not require creating a special mental state or focusing intensely.
- Give yourself permission to simply be who you already are in the present moment.
- Let go of the need to think specific thoughts, avoid thoughts, or become anyone else.
- Understand that true "holding yourself" means being alert and aware that you are aware, without any agenda.

## Welcoming Resistance and Emotions
- Notice what emotions, sensations, or resistance arise while you are holding yourself.
- Locate the specific physical sensation or emotion within your body and breathe into that space.
- Ask yourself: Can you welcome and accept this moment and the resistance that is present?
- Practice graduated acceptance: If 100% acceptance is not possible, try to welcome the sensation 20% or 50%, acknowledging that whatever level you achieve is okay.

## Core Reflection Questions
During the practice, ask yourself the following:
- "Can you welcome this moment? Can you accept this moment?"
- "What are you feeling right now? What state is coming up as you are holding yourself?"
- "Where in the body are you feeling a resistance, a sensation, or an emotion?"
- "Can you welcome it 20%? Maybe 50%? Maybe 100%?"
