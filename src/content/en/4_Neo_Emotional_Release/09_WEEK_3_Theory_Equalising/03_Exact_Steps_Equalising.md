# Exact Steps of Equalising

Equalising is the first step in gaining access to the subconscious. It involves addressing the undefined energy or resistance present in a person's body and inviting them to accept it without judgment. This simple communication principle helps to bridge the gap between the ego and the body, creating space for whatever resistance or emotion is present to simply exist.

## What is Equalising?
* It is the verbal expression of the "master state" — communicating earnestly and without force.
* It serves as an invitation to let go of resistance to the present moment, bodily sensations, or specific situations.
* The goal is to ask the client to witness, feel, or let go of their internal resistance.

## The Core Technique: Phrasing the Question
* Equalising relies on asking genuine, non-rhetorical questions to invite acceptance.
* It is not a command, but a sincere inquiry into whether the client can or cannot allow what is happening.
* **Examples of Equalising Questions:**
  * "Can you allow this resistance to be there?"
  * "Can you welcome it?"
  * "Can you allow, can you witness this?"

## Applications of Equalising
Equalising can be applied to any form of resistance, as the ego is almost always resisting reality. Asking these questions consistently creates a shift in the client.
* **Common scenarios include:**
  * **Resistance to an emotion:** "Can you allow yourself to feel the resistance you have towards your own sadness?"
  * **Resistance to a situation:** "Can you allow that you feel your mother is angry at you?"
  * **Resistance to physical sensations:** "Can you allow that your leg is falling asleep right now?"
  * **Resistance to positive states:** "Can you allow to feel the strength that is also inside of you?"

## The Finisher Move: Somatic Resonance
* The most profound application of equalising involves inviting the entire body to resonate with the emotion or resistance.
* You can gently guide the client to expand the feeling somatically:
  * "Can you allow your entire body to resonate with that resistance?"
  * "Can you allow your arm to feel this emotion?"
  * "Can you allow your legs to feel this emotion?"
* This builds up the invitation and fully involves the somatic experience in the process of letting go.
