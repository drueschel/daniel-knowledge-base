# The 3 Big Mistakes of Master State (Holding Space)

The "Master State" is a foundational principle of healing in Neo-Emotional Release, defined simply as unconditional space-holding. True space-holding goes beyond merely being present; it requires a deep, non-judgmental allowance for individuals to feel emotions they have long suppressed. Mastering this state takes time, as it demands balancing empathetic connection with personal boundaries.

## The Roots of Emotional Suppression
* **The Illusion of Problems:** Most issues presented by clients are not logical problems to be solved, but rather unfelt emotions. As a core principle states: "There are no problems, only emotions that you don't want to feel."
* **The Network of Shame:** Childhood conditioning often teaches us that certain emotions (vulnerability, anger, unbridled joy, sexuality) are unacceptable. Families enforce unspoken contracts of acceptable behavior through a subconscious "network of shame."
* **Impact on Self-Worth:** Suppressing authentic expression to maintain connection and acceptance creates deep internal resistance and a persistent sense of low self-worth ("I am not okay").
* **Healing Through Permission:** By maintaining Master State, you give clients the profound permission to express who they truly are. This melts away emotional resistance, often providing them with their first experience of unconditional acceptance.

## The 3 Big Mistakes of Holding Space

When attempting to hold space, practitioners commonly fall into one of three traps:

### 1. Staying on the Mental Level
* **The Mistake:** Treating the client's emotional suffering as a logical problem to fix by offering actionable advice or new mental perspectives.
* **The Impact:** This deflects from the core emotions and inadvertently validates the client's existing resistance to feeling them. It creates an environment that prevents true emotional release.

### 2. Over-Soothing and Over-Comforting
* **The Mistake:** Reacting to a client's tears or vulnerability with an overly motherly, comforting approach (e.g., "Oh, it's okay, don't cry").
* **The Impact:** Sometimes, crying and victimhood are defense mechanisms used to appear non-threatening, masking deeper feelings of helplessness or anger. Over-comforting reinforces this defense mechanism rather than allowing the authentic, underlying emotions to surface.

### 3. Lacking Energetic Boundaries
* **The Mistake:** Becoming overly involved in the client's emotions, feeling too much of what they feel, and trying to "save" them.
* **The Impact:** Practitioners lose touch with their own bodies, sacrificing their life energy and dignity. Effective space-holding requires managing your own energy independently from the client's.

## The Balance of Master State
Master State requires standing directly in the middle of two polarities:
1. **Disconnection:** Trying to logically solve the problem without emotional connection.
2. **Over-connection:** Losing boundaries, feeling too much of the client's pain, and trying to save them.

To hold space effectively, you must stay empathetically connected to the client's emotions while remaining firmly grounded within your own physical and energetic boundaries.
