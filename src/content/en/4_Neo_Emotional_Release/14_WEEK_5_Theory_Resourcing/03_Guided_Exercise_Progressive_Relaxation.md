# Guided Exercise: Progressive Relaxation

This guided practice focuses on working with the pelvic floor—one of the body's primary diaphragms—through the technique of progressive muscle relaxation. By consciously contracting and releasing physical tension, we can reconnect with our vital life force, unblock stagnant energy, and use somatic resourcing to cultivate a profound sense of ease and aliveness in the body.

## The Three Diaphragms and Bandhas
* The body features three basic diaphragms: the throat, the breathing diaphragm, and the pelvic floor (hip area).
* In yogic traditions, *bandhas* (locks) are used to intentionally contract and then release these areas, functioning similarly to progressive muscle relaxation.
* Because we often carry chronic, unconscious tension in the pelvic floor, we lose awareness of how it feels to be truly relaxed. This rigidity can restrict flexibility in multiple aspects of our lives.
* Actively training these muscles to contract and let go builds awareness and restores natural elasticity.

## The Guided Exercise: Pelvic Floor Release
* **Contract:** Take a deep breath in and intensely tense up the muscles of your pelvic floor. Pull everything tightly together.
* **Release:** Exhale slowly, sigh it out, and completely let go. Allow the muscles to relax fully.
* **Repeat:** Go through this cycle of intense contraction and full release three times, bringing deep awareness to the physical sensations of letting go.

## Resourcing the Pelvic Floor
Once the area is relaxed, stay present with the physical sensations and build a supportive somatic resource:
* Focus on the comfortable feeling in the area. How does it present itself? (e.g., tingling, warm, soft, colored).
* Imagine the entire area getting softer, heavier, and opening up as it sinks down.
* Offer a smile, words of affirmation, or gratitude to the area.
* **Observe the response:** Does the area answer with a feeling, a memory, a picture, or a sound?
* Ask yourself: *What kind of resources does this area have for you? What potential or power lies within it?*

## Reconnecting to Vital Life Force
* Releasing tension in the pelvic floor often triggers a flow of vital energy that can feel sensual or rousing.
* This is our inherent life force. It is not necessarily "sexual" in a needy or goal-oriented way, but rather the fundamental energy of feeling alive, ecstatic, and joyful.
* Due to shame or negative conditioning around sexuality, we often unconsciously block this life force, which simultaneously blocks our capacity for joy and pleasure in everyday life.
* The goal of somatic resourcing is to deliberately reconnect to areas of comfort and ease within the body, reminding our system of what it feels like to let this vital energy flow freely.
