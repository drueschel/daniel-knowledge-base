# Introduction to Resourcing

In this lesson, David Manning introduces the concept of resourcing, a foundational technique in Neo-Emotional Release (NER) that focuses on re-establishing a sense of safety, comfort, and pleasantness within the body.

## All Qualities Are Always Present
* **Inherent Wholeness:** All human qualities—vulnerability, strength, sadness, joy, and safety—always exist within our awareness.
* **Trauma's Impact:** Trauma causes the mind to block out certain qualities. The shutdown reaction, fear, and overwhelm can cause us to lose touch with our inherent sense of safety.
* **Reclaiming Qualities:** By intentionally focusing our mind on specific aspects of ourselves, we can reclaim and awaken these qualities, deepening our connection to them.

## The Power of Resourcing
* **Establishing Safety:** Resourcing involves deliberately focusing on aspects of safety, comfort, and pleasantness in the body. This process re-establishes safety in the nervous system and rewires the brain to feel safer.
* **Integrating the Uncomfortable:** Once safety is established, we can gently introduce uncomfortable emotions and sensations back into the system.
* **Rewiring the Nervous System:** Experiencing both safety and the uncomfortable sensation simultaneously fundamentally rewires the nervous system. It teaches the body that previously "unwelcome" emotions, like shame, are now allowed to exist alongside safety.
* **A Gentle Approach:** Resourcing allows for a gentle approach to healing. We do not need a masochistic approach, nor do we need to force ourselves into painful, shattering processes. We can meet our discomfort from a place of safety and gentleness.

## Overcoming Shame and Guilt
* **Restoring Dignity:** Shame and guilt can estrange us from our natural sense of dignity and respect.
* **Honoring Our Rhythm:** When feeling worthless or shameful, we might try to force healing to become "more worthy," ignoring our natural rhythm. Resourcing helps us stay within the bounds of our own safety, respecting our nervous system's capacity.

## Pendulation in Facilitation
* **Continuous Safety:** Resourcing is crucial at the beginning of a facilitation session and throughout, providing a safe anchor to return to at any time.
* **Pendulation:** This technique, often called "pendulation," involves moving back and forth between an uncomfortable emotion and the state of safety, allowing for gradual integration and healing.
