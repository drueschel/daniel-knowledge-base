# Somatic Evolution: What Does an NER Session Look Like?

This lesson breaks down the anatomy of a Neo-Emotional Release (NER) session through a practical example between a practitioner (Sophie) and her client (Leana). It demonstrates how to seamlessly transition from establishing basic safety and grounding to entering deep childhood trauma containers, tracking physical, emotional, and visual cues simultaneously.

## 1. Establishing Safety and Grounding
- **Emotional Release Breath (ERB):** The session begins by establishing the foundational breathing technique.
- **Visualization for Grounding:** Instead of pure body awareness, the practitioner uses visualization (imagining a safe place like a forest or desert) to help the client sink into the ground and allow calmness to land in the body.
- **Surrender:** The goal is to let the feeling of security flood the body, allowing the client to fully surrender to the moment.

## 2. Basic Connection and Physical Activation
- **Heart Connection:** The practitioner connects with the client's heart to establish basic connection, inviting the client to bring their awareness to the point of contact.
- **Inviting Sensations:** The client is encouraged to invite all sensations, whether positive or negative. Tingling in hands and feet is normalized as energy exiting the body.
- **Physical Touch and Pressure:** Based on intuitive reading of the client and tissue palpation, the practitioner places a hand on a heavy or painful area. 
- **Sound and Release:** As the client breathes out with sound, the energetic system opens, allowing the practitioner to incrementally increase physical pressure to help energy flow.

## 3. The Doorway Approach
- **Converting Sensation to Visualization:** A physical sensation is successfully converted into a visual doorway. 
- **Assessing the Door:** The appearance of the door provides clues about the trauma. For example, a very old door might indicate ancestral or generational trauma.

## 4. Navigating the "Soul Vision" and Somatic Release
- **Anchoring in the Scene:** The practitioner asks the client to look at their body and clothes in the visualization, anchoring them firmly in the "Soul Vision."
- **Exploring Resistance:** When the client experiences a "waiting" feeling, the practitioner identifies it as a resistance to the present moment and encourages expanding the underlying insecurity and shame.
- **Exaggerating Tension:** The client is instructed to press their lips together to physically exaggerate and connect with the feeling of holding back.
- **Diaphragm Vibration:** The practitioner uses a vibrating motion on the belly to push the diaphragm slightly, encouraging energy and air to move upward.
- **Release Reactions:** The client's hands become stiff and frozen. The practitioner normalizes this as a common release reaction, often linked to helplessness or powerlessness, and instructs the client to simply breathe into the stiffness.

## 5. Entering the Trauma Container
- **Transitioning the Scene:** The visual door transforms into the door of the client's childhood room, indicating entry into the trauma container.
- **Inner Child Connection:** The client connects with their 7 or 8-year-old self. The practitioner ensures the client is not just visually in the room but physically feeling the experience in their body.
- **Mirroring and Expansion:** The client connects with the feeling of being alone while hearing family tension outside the room. The practitioner mirrors these feelings back to re-establish the emotion, allowing the feeling of helplessness and tension to fully expand so it can be released.
