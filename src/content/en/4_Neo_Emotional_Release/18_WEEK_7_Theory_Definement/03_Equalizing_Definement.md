# Equalizing and Definement

Equalizing resistance is a powerful tool, but when that resistance holds a massive amount of energy, simply welcoming it may not be enough. In these moments, we introduce the principle of Definement—identifying and naming the underlying emotion to create safety, reduce resistance, and allow for deeper processing.

## The Limits of Equalizing

Equalizing involves inviting the ego to drop its resistance to the present moment, the body, or reality itself. By asking questions like, "Can you just for a moment welcome that this is difficult?", we help clients reach deep states of relaxation and allow difficult emotions to surface.

However, when the resistance carries a very high energetic charge, the client will eventually hit a limit. While they might be able to "allow that it is difficult to allow," the sheer volume of energy requires a more precise approach to fully release the blockage.

## Definement: Naming the Resistance

Instead of trying to release a massive block of energy bit by bit, we explore the nature of the resistance itself. Resistance is, fundamentally, an emotion or stored energy.

By asking, "What emotion is this resistance?", we direct the client's awareness deeper into their body. When the brain successfully identifies and defines the specific emotion (e.g., recognizing it as anger), something clicks. The ego understands itself better, which immediately creates a sense of safety in the body and causes a portion of the resistance to drop away.

## Going Deeper into the Trauma Container

Definement allows us to access the inner dimensions of a person's experience. Once an emotion is identified, we can alternate back to Equalizing: "Can you welcome that there's anger there?"

From there, Definement can continue to uncover the roots held within the trauma container:
*   "Is this anger related to a male or female person?"
*   "Is it your dad?"

As the specific information held within the blockage is revealed and defined, the capacity to host the emotional charge increases. The energy dissipates, and the blockage shrinks.

## Practical Exercises & Questions for Definement

Clients often struggle to accurately identify their own emotions when in a triggered or resistant state. As a practitioner, you can guide them by making gentle suggestions:

*   **Suggesting Emotions:** "Is this emotion you're feeling fear? No? Is it anger? Yes."
*   **Navigating Mixed Emotions:** If a client is feeling overwhelmed by multiple feelings, break it down. "Maybe you're feeling two emotions. One is sadness. What is the other? You don't know... is the other emotion anger? Yes."
*   **Equalizing the Mix:** "So you're feeling sadness and anger at the same time. Can you allow yourself to feel sadness and anger at the same time?"
