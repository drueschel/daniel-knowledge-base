# Definement: Exact Steps

The principle of Definement in Neo-Emotional Release is a powerful tool to facilitate deeper emotional processing. By helping the client clearly identify and articulate what they are feeling, we unlock the information stored within the emotion, allowing for profound healing. Definement is not just a technique; it is a fundamental principle that states: the more clearly an emotion is defined, the deeper it can be processed.

## The Process of Definement

* **Identify the Resistance as an Emotion:** When a client is in touch with resistance, the first step is to recognize it as an emotion. Ask, "What emotion is this resistance?" Identifying it as anger, sadness, fear, etc., is the first basic definement.
  * *Tip:* If the client struggles, you can offer suggestions like, "Are you feeling fear at the moment?"
* **Identify the Relational Context:** Since a high percentage of our emotions are related to other people, determining the relational context is crucial.
  * Ask, "Is this emotion related to a male or female person?"
  * **The Law of Resonance:** The client will know the answer intuitively. When something is true, it resonates within the body. We don't make suggestions to convince them; we rely on their bodily reaction to the truth.
* **Narrow Down the Specific Person:** Once the gender is identified, keep defining.
  * If female: "Is it your mom?" or "Is it your grandma?"
  * If male: "Is it your dad?" or "Is it a partner/friend?"
  * *Why this works:* Every piece of accurate information stripped from the emotion deepens the processing.

## Additional Definement Inquiries

Beyond asking who the emotion is related to, you can use definement in several other powerful ways to unpack the trauma container:

* **Temporal Definement:** Ask, "When is the last time you've felt this emotion?" This guides the client into their memory, allowing more information to surface.
* **Somatic Definement:** Ask, "Where do you feel this emotion in the body?" Locating the emotion can be incredibly helpful. Specific body parts often correlate with specific relationships (e.g., the left shoulder or upper arm is frequently related to a younger sibling).
* **Process Definement:** Ask, "What has changed in the body?" Asking a client to describe the shifts they are experiencing helps to integrate and process the healing.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Describing helps the healing process. We are moving along the inner dimensions of another human being, and bringing conscious definition to their experience allows it to integrate and resolve.
