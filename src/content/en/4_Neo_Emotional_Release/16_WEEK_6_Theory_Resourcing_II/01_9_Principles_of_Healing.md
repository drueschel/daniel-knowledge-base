# 9 Principles of Healing

In the practice of Neo-Emotional Release, true transformation relies on creating a safe space for the body's innate wisdom to emerge. These nine fundamental principles guide facilitators in supporting clients through their emotional processing without trying to "fix" them.

## 1. The Relationship to the Emotion is Key
It matters less which specific emotion arises and more how the client relates to it. If someone suppresses anger or denies sadness, the facilitator's role is to create safety, grant permission to express the unexpressed, and shift their relationship to that feeling.

## 2. Re-Experiencing with Safety and Support
Traumatic imprints are always linked to a loss of power, safety, and support. Re-experiencing these past events in the present moment—while having the safety and support that was originally missing—rewires neural pathways and shifts the perspective on what once felt unbearable.

## 3. Facilitation, Not Solving
It is not your job to "fix" or solve the person. You are there simply to facilitate the process. Whatever needs to happen will happen—nothing more, nothing less.

## 4. The Body Wants to Heal
The human body possesses a desperate, natural drive toward healing. Our primary task is simply to get out of the way and remove the barriers that prevent it.

## 5. Healing Means Witnessing
Healing occurs when we can finally witness what previously could not be witnessed. We can only heal what we are willing to feel and observe.

## 6. Welcoming the Unwelcome
Allowing previously unwelcome thoughts, feelings, and emotions to surface is a primary mechanism of healing. Doing so fundamentally transforms the relationship we have with ourselves and our emotions.

## 7. Presence of the Heart
As long as you remain present with your heart, you cannot do anything wrong. This work is a conscious exploration without a rigid goal. It requires surrendering to the same profound intelligence that grows trees and forms a child in the womb.

## 8. Awareness is Curative
Awareness alone is deeply healing. Just being present and aware changes how the ego reacts, embodying the core principles of mindfulness and meditation.

## 9. Breaking the Pattern
Repeating the same reactions keeps us stuck in loops. As facilitators, interrupting these automatic reaction patterns ("doing the opposite") unlocks hidden internal resources, giving the client the opportunity to finally break out of old cycles.
