# What Does an NER Session Look Like? (Demo Part 1)

In this demonstration, we observe the foundational steps of a Neo-Emotional Release (NER) session. The practitioner focuses on guiding the client into their body, establishing safety, and utilizing somatic techniques to navigate from physical sensations into the deeper, emotional "trauma container." The session beautifully highlights how breathwork, visualization, and intuitive touch weave together to safely surface and process stored emotions.

## Establishing Safety and Grounding
- **The Emotional Release of Breath:** The session begins immediately with conscious, continuous breathing (in and out through the mouth) to calm the nervous system and anchor the client.
- **Visualization:** Instead of purely physical body awareness, the practitioner uses visualization (e.g., imagining a safe place like a forest) to help the client surrender, sink into the ground, and experience a deep sense of security.

## Somatic Connection and Physical Activation
- **Basic Connection:** The practitioner connects with the client's heart, inviting them to bring their awareness to the feeling of aliveness under the practitioner's hand. 
- **Welcoming Sensations:** Tingling in the hands and feet is normalized as a sign of energy exiting the body. The client is encouraged to invite in all sensations, whether they feel positive or challenging.
- **Intuitive Touch and Pressure:** By intuitively placing a hand on an area of heaviness and increasing pressure during the exhale, the practitioner helps open the energetic system, allowing stored pain or tension to flow more freely.

## The Doorway Approach and Trauma Container
- **Entering the Subconscious:** The practitioner uses the "Doorway Approach," inviting the client to visualize a door. The material and age of the door can provide clues about the nature or ancestral roots of the trauma.
- **Anchoring the Soul Vision:** To fully immerse the client in the experience, the practitioner asks them to describe their physical appearance (clothes, hands, age) in the visualization.
- **Navigating Resistance:** When the client feels stuck in a "waiting position," this is recognized as a resistance to the present moment. The practitioner guides the client to lean into the insecurity and physical tension (e.g., pressing lips together) to exaggerate and fully express the suppressed emotion, often linked to shame or a "fight" response.
- **Release Reactions:** Physical stiffness or "freezing" in the hands is identified as a common release reaction associated with helplessness and powerlessness.
- **Stepping into the Memory:** The door transforms into the client's childhood room. Connecting with her 7-year-old self, the client uncovers core feelings of aloneness and helplessness amid family tension. The practitioner actively mirrors these feelings to help the client fully expand and process the emotional state.
