# Week 1 Demo: Introduction and Logging In

In this opening demonstration of the Neo-Emotional Release (NER) process, we cover the foundational steps of starting a session: checking in with the client, establishing the practitioner's Master State, and guiding the client into their body through a process called "Logging In".

## Checking In With the Client
The session begins with a brief check-in to understand the client's current state, including any struggles or physical sensations they are experiencing. 
* **Mirroring and Precision:** It is crucial to use the exact wording of the client (e.g., "tension in the lower belly") without making assumptions or adding interpretations.
* **Keep It Simple:** Often, you do not need extensive information; a clear physical or emotional reference point is enough to begin the work.

## Establishing the Master State
Before diving deeper, the practitioner must cultivate a specific state of awareness.
* **Self-Awareness:** Start by closing your eyes and turning inward. Observe your own bodily sensations, thoughts, doubts, and emotions (nervousness, excitement, curiosity) without judgment. This ensures that you act from a place of trust and inner peace, preventing your own state from manipulating the session.
* **Connected Awareness:** Slowly extend your awareness to the client. Take in their story, body language, and presence with pure compassion. The goal is simply to be present, without any urge to judge or fix.
* **The Master State:** This profound state is achieved when your deep connection to yourself (self-awareness) seamlessly merges with your awareness of the client. 

## Logging In
Once the Master State is established, the next step is to help the client "log in." This means shifting their attention away from the external world and the analytical mind, and bringing them into a state of witnessing their internal bodily sensations.

### Exercise: Guided Body Scan (Logging In)
Use this gentle script to guide your client into their body:
* Instruct the client to take a deep breath in and exhale with a sigh through the mouth. Repeat this a few times.
* Ask them to tune into their body, starting with the points touching the ground and feeling their weight.
* Guide their awareness slowly upward through the body: feet, toes, ankles, calves, knees, thighs, and hips.
* Move attention to the pelvic area, lower abdomen, chest, heart area, lungs, and the movement of the diaphragm with every breath.
* Direct relaxation through the back, shoulders, arms, hands, and fingers.
* Finish by relaxing the neck, face (mouth, ears, eyes, nose, forehead), and the entire head.
