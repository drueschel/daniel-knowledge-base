# Psychosomatics | The Significance of Fascia

## Key Concepts
- **Transitioning to Neo-Emotional Release:** Moving beyond purely physical sensations into the emotional realm by identifying the emotion connected to a specific sensation.
- **Somatic Integration Process:** The foundational steps of the practice include localizing, describing, equalizing, and monitoring the physical sensation.
- **Defining Emotion with Sound:** A powerful technique where the client connects a sound (high or low) to the emotion and expresses it vocally to facilitate deep emotional processing.
- **Practitioner Resonance:** If a client struggles to name the emotion, the practitioner can offer suggestions based on their own resonance and intuition.

## Summary
In this week seven practice session of the Somatic Evolution course, the focus shifts from somatic integration to the deeper work of Neo-Emotional Release. The exercise begins with connected awareness, where the client is invited to bring up a problem or resistance and locate the corresponding physical sensation in their body. From there, the practitioner guides them through the standard somatic integration steps: localizing, describing, equalizing, and monitoring the sensation. 

Once the sensation is stabilized, the process moves into the emotional realm by asking the client what emotion is connected to the physical feeling. If the client is unsure, the practitioner uses their resonance to gently suggest emotions like anger, fear, or guilt until one resonates. The emotion is then localized, described, and equalized, just as with physical sensations. 

The core of this practice is defining the emotion with sound. The client is asked to imagine if the emotion were to vibrate, whether it would make a high or low sound, and is then encouraged to express and hold that sound for at least 20 seconds. This simple yet profound tool creates a significant impact on the emotional realm, often shifting the emotion or bringing up new sensations, allowing the cycle of integration to continue.
