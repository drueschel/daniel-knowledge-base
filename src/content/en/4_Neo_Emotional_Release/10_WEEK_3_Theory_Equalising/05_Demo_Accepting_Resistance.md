# Demo: Accepting Resistance

In this demonstration, David guides a client (Dennis) through the process of accepting resistance and uncomfortable emotions linked to attention and focus issues. By systematically identifying and welcoming physical sensations and their underlying emotions, the client learns to release the resistance that constructs the "problem," thereby accessing a renewed source of energy and hope.

## The Illusion of the "Problem"
- **Mind's Construction:** The mind often constructs a "problem" out of reality by comparing the current state (e.g., lack of focus) with an idealized future state (e.g., high productivity).
- **Rejecting Reality:** The core issue is not the lack of focus itself, but the refusal to accept the present reality and the emotions associated with it. "You don't actually have a problem. You only have emotions that you don't want to feel."
- **Goal:** To find happiness and acceptance in the present moment, without needing external circumstances to change.

## The Process of Equalising
- **Locating Sensation:** The process begins by identifying where the struggle is felt in the body (e.g., an "airy" or "constricted" feeling in the chest).
- **Incremental Acceptance:** Welcoming the physical sensation gradually (10%, 20%, 30%, etc.) helps bypass initial cognitive resistance.
- **Reaching the Threshold:** As acceptance increases, the underlying emotional resistance often reveals itself naturally (e.g., worry or fear).
- **Welcoming Underlying Emotions:** The newly revealed emotion (fear) is located in the body and welcomed. If that uncovers another layer (like sadness or emptiness), that too is embraced.
- **Meta-Acceptance:** If an emotion is too difficult to accept, you can ask the client to *welcome that it is difficult to welcome*.

## Finding the Enjoyable
- **Shifting Perspective:** Asking the client to find something enjoyable or positive about the feeling (e.g., the hope of uncovering true authenticity underneath the struggle).
- **Expanding the Positive:** Welcoming and expanding that positive aspect connects the client to a hidden source of energy.
- **Integration:** This newly released, tingling energy is then used to hold and welcome the remaining sadness or resistance.

## Accepting the Struggle
- **Revisiting the Original Issue:** After releasing energy, the client is asked again if they can accept their initial condition (e.g., being unfocused).
- **Stopping the War:** Even if the issue still feels like a struggle, accepting *that* it is a struggle is a crucial step. Choosing to be "less at war with the struggle" inherently frees up more physical and emotional energy.
