# Introduction into Equalising

The ego operates as a "resistance machine" to reality, constantly strategizing for survival and creating loops of resistance. In Neo-Emotional Release (NER), the technique of **Equalising** offers a profound antidote: the practice of unconditional acceptance. By simply allowing a person to be exactly who they are in the present moment without forcing change, profound healing can occur.

## The Ego as a Resistance Machine
- The ego's primary function is survival, which means it constantly strategizes and resists reality as it is.
- This creates madness through looping mechanisms—for example, having fear about experiencing fear, which escalates into a panic attack.
- True healing requires the opposite: stepping out of this echo chamber of resistance.

## The Principle of Equalising
- Equalising is a foundational language principle in NER that embodies unconditional acceptance.
- It is a deeply feminine, allowing energy that invites the client to be more themselves than they have ever permitted.
- Instead of demanding change, the therapist simply asks: *"Can you be who you are right now? Can you welcome that it is there?"*

## Avoiding Transference and Counter-Transference
- When a therapist pushes a client to "let go" or resolve a problem too quickly, they are subtly rejecting the client's present reality.
- This creates a discrepancy where the client feels they "need to be someone else" to be accepted.
- Moving too fast projects an energy of non-acceptance, causing the client to feel unsafe and triggering transference or counter-transference.

## Practical Demonstration
During a session, a client described feeling drained and unseen when their partner spoke at length. 

The Equalising process unfolded as follows:
- **Identifying the Goal:** The client wanted to feel seen, rather than trying to control or change the partner.
- **Meeting Resistance:** When asked to allow the partner's need for energy, the client felt resistance.
- **Equalising the Resistance:** Instead of pushing past the block, the therapist asked the client to accept the resistance itself: *"Can you welcome that it's difficult to allow another person to take that energy from you?"*
- **Deepening Acceptance:** When the client found even that difficult, the therapist layered the equalising: *"Can you allow it to be difficult to accept that it's difficult?"*
- **The Result:** By simply accepting the layers of resistance, the client immediately felt a profound sense of energetic relief in their body.
