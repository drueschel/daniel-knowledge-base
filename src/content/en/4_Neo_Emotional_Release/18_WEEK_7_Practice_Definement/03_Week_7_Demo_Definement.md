# Week 7 Demo | Definement

## Key Concepts
- The limitation of simply equalizing (welcoming resistance) when emotional energy is very high.
- Using "definement" to explore and identify the exact emotion behind the resistance.
- How naming the emotion creates safety in the body, allowing deeply suppressed content to surface.
- Progressively defining the emotion further (e.g., linking it to a specific person) to dissipate energy and reduce blockages.
- The technique of making emotional suggestions when the client struggles to identify what they are feeling.

## Summary
This demonstration explores how to transition from the principle of "equalizing" to "definement" when dealing with highly charged emotional resistance. While asking questions to welcome resistance is powerful, it can reach a limit when the underlying energy is too intense. Instead of trying to slowly release it, the practitioner invites the client to identify the emotion that constitutes the resistance, prompting a deeper inward focus.

By successfully identifying and naming the emotion, such as anger or fear, the ego understands itself better. This cognitive recognition generates safety within the body, which naturally diminishes the initial resistance and opens the door to deeper subconscious content. The practitioner can then guide the client to define the emotion even further, such as tracing it back to a specific person or event, helping to unlock and dissipate the emotional charge within the trauma container.

Additionally, the lesson highlights practical strategies for when a client is unable to articulate their feelings. The practitioner can offer gentle suggestions to help them pinpoint the emotion, or break down complex emotional states into multiple distinct feelings, ultimately guiding them to welcome the full spectrum of their present experience.
