# Demo: Definement to Sounding

This session demonstrates the practical application of the Definement phase, focusing on how to guide a client to locate physical resistance and identify the underlying repressed emotion. The key objective is to uncover the emotion the client has the most difficult relationship with, as this is where true transformation occurs.

## Core Concepts & Process

- **Locating the Resistance:**
  - Begin by asking the client where they feel resistance or a specific sensation in their body.
  - Encourage them to physically point to the "epicenter" of the sensation to establish a clear connection to the body (e.g., chest area, ribcage).

- **Identifying the Emotion:**
  - Ask the client what emotion they are feeling in relation to the physical resistance.
  - Assess their relationship to the stated emotion. Is it easily accessible, or is it usually bottled up?
  - *Crucial insight:* The emotion you are looking for is the one the person has a *bad relationship* with. If an emotion is readily available, it is already free and does not need to be released. We are looking for what is being held back.

- **Testing Different Emotions:**
  - If the client immediately names an emotion (like anger), it may not be the hidden, transformative emotion. Be gently skeptical and suggest other possibilities.
  - Guide them to breathe into different emotions and observe the physical shifts (e.g., "Are you feeling sadness?", "Can you welcome guilt?").
  - Notice the somatic reactions. In the demonstration, welcoming guilt created the most significant physiological shift (warmth, tingling, and subsequent relaxation).

- **The Nature of Repressive Forces:**
  - Emotions like guilt, shame, and fear act as repressive forces that "split" the nervous system.
  - When these emotions are allowed and welcomed, they dissolve back into pure life force. The body stops fragmenting and begins to return to a state of peace and relaxation.

## Exercise: Exploring Emotional Matches

- **The Practice:** Pair up and use the mechanism of asking, "What emotion are you feeling?"
- **Guidance:** If the partner is unsure or doesn't know what they are feeling, gently make suggestions by going through a list of core emotions (anger, sadness, shame, guilt, fear).
- **Observation:** Notice that people may resonate with multiple emotions. Your goal is to find the emotion that is the *most held back*, as improving the relationship with this specific emotion will be the most transformative for their system.
