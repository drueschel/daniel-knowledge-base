# What Does an NER Session Look Like (Part 2)

This transcript breaks down the second part of a Neo-Emotional Release (NER) demonstration, focusing on the client's transition from a state of helplessness into empowerment, and finally into deep somatic integration.

## Stepping Out of the Familiar

- **Leaving the "Childhood Room":** Clients often find comfort in familiar states of helplessness or sadness. True emotional release requires stepping out of this "childhood room" to access deeper, more transformative emotions like anger or personal power.
- **Somatic Expressions of Stuckness:**
  - **Knees:** Represent stability and the ability to stand strong.
  - **Hands:** Symbolize holding on, being unable to let go, or feeling bound by responsibility.
  - **Shoulders/Traps:** Where the weight of responsibility is carried. Applying pressure here can help disperse the energy of the "role ego."

## Activating Personal Power

- **Overcoming the Fear of Greatness:** There is often intense resistance in the body to stepping into one's own power. The ego can perceive strength and greatness as dangerous or threatening.
- **Activating the "Yes":** Being stuck in a victim state means saying "no" to oneself. The therapist guides the client to activate an inner "yes" using empowering vocalizations (e.g., "I am [Name], and I can decide").
- **Working with Shame:** Shame is deeply connected to the face and how we show ourselves to the world. As energy moves and shame is processed, facial muscles begin to warm up and release.

## The Dual Role of the Therapist

A skilled NER practitioner fluidly transitions between archetypal energies to support the client's process:
- **The Father Energy:** Pushing the client, activating their power, and challenging their limitations.
- **The Mother Energy:** Nurturing, holding space, and allowing the client to surrender into feeling small, held, and safe.

## Returning Home to the Body

The culmination of the session is a profound energetic shift. The client transitions from feeling trapped and stiff to feeling lively and vibrant. This somatic evolution is often visibly apparent (e.g., changes in skin color) and signifies a true return "home" to the body.
