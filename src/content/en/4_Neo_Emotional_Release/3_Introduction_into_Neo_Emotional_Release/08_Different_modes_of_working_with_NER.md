# Different Modes of Working with NER

The core of Neo-Emotional Release (NER) begins with the "Master State"—maintaining a stable presence with oneself and expanding it to co-regulate others. True emotional release is not measured by the volume of screaming or the amount of tears, but rather by how we transform our relationship with uncomfortable emotions and our ability to witness the parts of ourselves we previously avoided.

## The Three Levels of Awareness
NER integrates three evolutionary layers of human experience. Working on just one level misses the full potential for healing, so all three must be integrated:
* **Somatic (Sensations):** The oldest evolutionary layer, primarily regulated in the brainstem. This includes basic physical sensations like tingling, physical touch, or dryness.
* **Emotional:** Regulated in the limbic system, this is where specific emotional feelings reside.
* **Visual & Symbolic:** The most recently developed centers, responsible for dreams and visual imagination. 

## Somatic Integration as the Foundation
* **Gentle Yet Powerful:** Somatic integration serves as a gentle, beginner-friendly mode (like putting on "swimming floats") but remains highly effective in advanced sessions.
* **Sensational Focus:** It focuses primarily on the sensational level, serving as the starting point for somatic evolution.
* **The Core Process:** Establish a safe therapeutic connection, create a space of inner awareness, and allow uncomfortable sensations and emotions to be witnessed without resistance.

## The Energetic Equation: Plus and Minus
* **Energy Drainers (Minus):** Resistance to uncomfortable sensations, emotions, or reality drains energy and causes the body to close. Addictive habits, toxic environments, and emotional suppression fall into this category.
* **Energy Givers (Plus / Resources):** Good habits, supportive relationships, psychological strengths, and safe environments give you energy.
* **Opening the Body:** When a facilitator provides safety and courage, the body opens, allowing suppressed resistances and emotions (like anger or shame) to surface organically.

## The Role of the Facilitator
* **Creating Permission:** The facilitator extends permission for the client to be authentic and integrates their identity.
* **Providing Safety:** By offering acceptance and safety, facilitators help clients feel their shame and difficult emotions instead of suppressing them.
* **Improving Vagal Tone:** This process raises the client's regulation level (often referred to as raising their "vibration"), which translates physiologically to an improved vagal tone.

## 💡 Self-Reflection Exercise
What are your top two channels of awareness? 
* Are you primarily **somatic** (noticing physical sensations like an itch or pain)? 
* Are you **emotional** (feeling rapid shifts in emotions)? 
* Or are you **visual** (seeing symbols and vivid imagery in your mind)?

*Note: Identifying and strengthening your relationship with your **least dominant channel** can often be the key to unlocking profound breakthroughs.*
