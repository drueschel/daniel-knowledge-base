# 01 Emotional Regulation

## Core Statements
- **Mental Health = Emotional Health:** Our mental health is largely determined by our emotional health (affect health).
- **Bottom-Up vs. Top-Down:** Bottom-up therapy (from body to brain) is often more effective than top-down therapy (talk therapy). The vagus nerve consists of 80% afferent fibers (signals from body to brain) and only 20% efferent fibers. We are essentially "20% thinking and 80% feeling."
- **Regulate -> Relate -> Reason:** According to the neurosequential model by Dr. Bruce Perry, we must first *regulate* before we can *relate*, and only then can we rationally *reason*. You cannot rationally discuss things with a dysregulated person.
- **Dysregulation & Trauma:** Emotional dysregulation is the inability to manage emotions healthily—often caused by trauma. When we are dysregulated (fight-or-flight mode), the limbic system blocks the prefrontal cortex (our rational thinking).
- **Healing:** Better emotional regulation means emotional triggers are less intense, occur less often, and have a shorter duration.

## Transcript Summary
In this lesson, the speaker explains the fundamental importance of the nervous system, drawing on experiences in neurology. An intact body is useless if nerve signals are blocked—and the same applies to our psyche.

He contrasts classical psychotherapy (top-down) with somatic work (bottom-up). Because the body sends far more data to the brain than vice versa (80% vs. 20%), the somatic approach provides a much larger lever to help someone out of a depression, for example.

Emotional regulation simply means "feeling your feelings"—it is the nervous system's ability to navigate the highs and lows of activation and return to calm. When we are not regulated, we make poor decisions, create drama, and isolate ourselves. The foundation of any healthy relationship and communication is to first regulate our own nervous system.

> *"You cannot reason with someone before you are regulated."*

## Theses
1. Working with the body (somatics) is the most effective way to change emotional and mental states.
2. Attempting to reach a dysregulated person with rational arguments is neurologically bound to fail.
